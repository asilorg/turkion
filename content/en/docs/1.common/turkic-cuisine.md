title: Turkic cuisine

**Turkic cuisine** is not a single cookbook but a family of related foodways shaped by pastoralism, oasis agriculture, trade routes, and empires from the steppe to Anatolia and the Caucasus. Ingredients and dishes overlap with Iranian, Mongol, Slavic, Chinese, and Mediterranean neighbors.

## Common traits

- **Grains**: wheat and rice as staples; noodles and stuffed pastries appear widely.
- **Meat**: lamb and beef predominate in many regions; horse meat is culturally significant in parts of Central Asia.
- **Dairy**: yogurt, *ayran*/*katyk*, clarified butter, dried curds (*qurt*), and fermented mare’s milk (*qymyz* / kumis) in pastoral zones.
- **Bread**: flatbreads baked in a *tandyr* / *tandır* appear from Central Asia to Anatolia under many local names.
- **Hospitality**: generous portions and repeated offerings of tea signal welcome.

## Regional clusters

- **Central Asian oasis and steppe**: rice pilafs, savory pastries (*samsa* / *somsa*), noodle soups (*lagman*), herb-heavy salads, preserved dairy.
- **South Caucasus (Azerbaijan)**: pilafs with herbs and dried fruits, dolma, grilled meats, tart soups.
- **Anatolia (Türkiye)**: Ottoman and regional Turkish repertoires — kebabs, börek, pilav, olive-oil vegetable dishes — interacting with Mediterranean and Balkan kitchens.

Modern urban diets everywhere combine traditional dishes with global fast food and café culture.

## Tea culture

Black tea, often strong and sweet in Central Asia and Azerbaijan, anchors daily social life. Türkiye favors brewed black tea in small glasses. Serving order and sweets paired with tea vary locally.
