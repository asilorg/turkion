---
title: Festivals and seasonal observances in Türkiye
description: An account of religious holidays, seasonal gatherings, and local fairs as cultural practices in Türkiye.
---

Festivals in Türkiye bring together religious calendars, agricultural seasons, municipal events, and family obligations. They are not a single national repertoire: observance changes by locality, household, generation, and religious affiliation. Visiting, food preparation, public entertainment, and market activity commonly connect formal holidays with ordinary social life.

## Historical and geographic context

Annual observances have developed through Islamic lunar dates, older seasonal calendars, local saintly associations, and the rhythms of planting, harvest, transhumance, and fishing. Urbanization has altered participation by concentrating work and education away from villages, while transport and media have extended the reach of particular events. Local fairs still preserve regional forms of exchange and performance.

## Ramazan Bayramı

*Ramazan Bayramı*, the festival following the Ramadan fast, is widely marked by prayer, cemetery visits, greeting elders, and visits among relatives and neighbors. Sweets, coffee, tea, and confectionery are offered to guests; children may receive money or treats. It is also called *Şeker Bayramı* in everyday speech, a name referring to sweets rather than a separate celebration.

Practices differ in timing and emphasis. In some communities, household visits begin immediately after prayer; elsewhere, families travel across long distances to assemble. Commercial districts, transport routes, and workplaces adjust schedules around the holiday, while village and neighborhood customs may organize visits by kin or age.

## Kurban Bayramı

*Kurban Bayramı* centers on sacrifice for households able and choosing to perform it, followed by the distribution and cooking of meat. The practice is governed by religious interpretation and practical arrangements that vary across locations. Meat may be shared with relatives, neighbors, and people in need, and dishes prepared during the holiday can include roasted, stewed, or preserved portions.

Family reunion is as significant as ritual procedure for many participants. Travel, hosting, greetings, and cemetery visits overlap with activities also found during Ramazan Bayramı. The holiday’s social meaning therefore extends beyond a uniform food or devotional practice.

## Nevruz and spring customs

In some eastern, southeastern, Alevi, and Turkic cultural settings, *Nevruz* marks the arrival of spring around 21 March. Gatherings can include food sharing, music, visiting, spring cleaning, and, where locally customary, outdoor fires or games. Its names and ceremonial interpretations differ, and participation is uneven across the country. For its wider regional context, see [Navruz](/en/docs/common/navruz).

Spring is also marked through local outings, picnics, and visits to places associated with water, trees, or tombs. These actions may be religious, recreational, familial, or several at once; treating every spring outing as the same ritual obscures local histories.

## Seasonal and local fairs

Harvest festivals, highland (*yayla*) gatherings, oil-wrestling events, camel-wrestling events, music festivals, and product fairs create regional calendars. Black Sea highland gatherings combine seasonal movement with markets and dance; Aegean fairs may feature agricultural products, animals, and wrestling. In coastal towns, fishing seasons and summer tourism can also shape public programming.

Such events include formal organizers but depend on vendors, performers, families, craft workers, and visitors. Their scale ranges from neighborhood celebrations to large commercialized festivals. Performance traditions discussed at these events are examined in [Music and dance in Türkiye](/en/docs/turkiye/music-dance).

## Variation

Holiday observance varies according to household resources, migration patterns, rural and urban residence, and community affiliation. A holiday visit may be a brief exchange in an apartment building, an extended journey to a hometown, or an occasion for public gathering. Seasonal events similarly combine inherited custom with new cultural and commercial formats.
