title: Uzbek cuisine
description: Representative dishes and culinary patterns of Uzbekistan, from pilaf and bread to soups, sweets, and beverages.
---

**Uzbek cuisine** reflects oasis farming, Silk Road trade, and Turkic-Persian synthesis. Meals are often communal; bread and tea carry strong symbolic value.

## Pilaf (*osh* / *palov*)

Rice cooked with oil, carrots, sometimes chickpeas or raisins, and layered meat (often lamb or beef) is the national centerpiece. Regional variants include **Fergana**, **Samarkand**, and **Bukhara** styles differing in grain texture, color, and aromatics.

## Breads (*non*)

Round flatbreads stamped with patterns and baked in a **tandyr** accompany nearly every meal. **Obi non** is a common variety; festive occasions may feature richer doughs.

## Savory pastries and starters

**Somsa** (baked or fried turnovers with meat, pumpkin, or greens) are everyday snacks. **Shashlik** (skewered grilled meat) appears in markets and restaurants.

## Soups and noodles

**Lagman** pulls hand-pulled noodles into a spiced broth with vegetables and meat. **Mastava** and **shurpa** are hearty soups blending rice or vegetables with lamb.

## Dairy and salads

**Katyk** (yogurt) and **suzma** (strained yogurt) cool spicy plates. Tomato-cucumber salads with dill or coriander balance rich mains.

## Sweets

**Halva**, **navat** sugar crystals, and dried fruits finish meals; many sweets intensify during holidays such as [Navruz](/en/docs/common/navruz).

## Beverages

Green tea dominates in many regions; black tea with sweets is common in cities. **Compote** from dried fruits appears seasonally.
