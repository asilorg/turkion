---
title: The Turkish War of Independence (1919–1923)
description: National struggle led from Ankara that blocked partition of Anatolia, abolished the Ottoman sultanate, and founded the Republic of Turkey.
---

## Historical setting

Defeat in the First World War brought the Ottoman Empire under Armistice of Mudros constraints and the prospect of partition embodied in Allied occupations and the Treaty of Sèvres (1920). Greek landing at Smyrna (Izmir) in May 1919 catalyzed Anatolian resistance. Mustafa Kemal (Atatürk) and associates organized a national movement centered on Ankara, asserting popular sovereignty against both occupying powers and the Istanbul sultanate’s compromised authority.

## Course of the war

The conflict comprised overlapping fronts: Greco-Turkish warfare in western Anatolia, Franco-Turkish fighting in the south, Armenian-Turkish clashes in the east, and political struggle with Istanbul. Victories such as Sakarya (1921) and the Great Offensive (1922) expelled Greek forces from Anatolia. Diplomatic settlement at Lausanne (1923) replaced Sèvres and internationally recognized the new state’s borders. Internally, the sultanate was abolished (1922) and the republic proclaimed (1923).

## Outcomes and significance

The war prevented the partition of Anatolian Turkish heartlands and reconstituted sovereignty as a secular nation-state rather than a dynastic Islamic empire. For Turkic history, it marks the principal twentieth-century reinvention of a major Turkic polity—shifting from imperial-millet frameworks toward nationalist citizenship—while remaining entangled with population exchanges, minority questions, and regional memory disputes.

## Historiographical notes

Republican historiography elevates national unity and leadership; Greek, Armenian, and other narratives emphasize catastrophe and displacement. Critical scholarship examines social mobilization, local violence, and the wartime origins of republican reforms without denying the conflict’s state-forming outcome.
