The **Turkish War of Independence** (15 May 1919 – 24 July 1923) was a series of military campaigns and a revolution waged by the [Turkish National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Movement), after the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) was occupied and [partitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_the_Ottoman_Empire) following its defeat in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I). The conflict was between the Turkish Nationalists against [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) and [separatist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolts_during_the_Turkish_War_of_Independence) forces over the application of [Wilsonian principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteen_Points), especially [self-determination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-determination), in post-World War I [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) and [eastern Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Thrace). The revolution concluded the [collapse of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Ottoman_Empire) and the [Eastern question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_question), ending the [Ottoman sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_Ottoman_sultanate) and the [Ottoman caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_Caliphate), and establishing the [Republic of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Turkey). This resulted in the transfer of sovereignty from the sultan-caliph to the [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_sovereignty), setting the stage for [nationalist revolutionary reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atat%C3%BCrk%27s_reforms) in Republican Turkey.

While World War I ended for the Ottomans with the [Armistice of Mudros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudros), the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) continued occupying land per the [Sykes–Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes%E2%80%93Picot_Agreement), and to facilitate the [prosecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecution_of_Ottoman_war_criminals_after_World_War_I) of former members of the [Committee of Union and Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress) and those involved in the [Armenian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide). Ottoman commanders therefore refused orders from the Allies and [Ottoman government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_late_Ottoman_Empire) to disband their forces. In an atmosphere of turmoil, Sultan [Mehmed VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_VI) dispatched general [Mustafa Kemal Pasha (Atatürk)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk), to restore order; however, he became an enabler and leader of [Turkish Nationalist resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Movement). In an attempt to establish control over the power vacuum in Anatolia, the Allies agreed to launch a Greek [peacekeeping force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_force) and [occupy Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Smyrna) ([İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmir)), inflaming sectarian tensions and beginning the Turkish War of Independence. A nationalist [counter government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Grand_National_Assembly) led by Mustafa Kemal was established in [Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara) when it became clear the Ottoman government was [appeasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeasement) the Allies. The Allies pressured the Ottoman "Istanbul government" to suspend the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Ottoman_Empire), [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_%28Ottoman_Empire%29), and sign the [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres), a treaty unfavorable to Turkish interests that the "[Ankara government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_government)" declared illegal.

[Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuva-yi_Milliye) forces defeated the [French in the south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Turkish_War), and remobilized army units went on to [partition Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_invasion_of_Armenia) with the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army), resulting in the [Treaty of Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kars) (1921). The Western Front is known as the [Greco-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_%281919%E2%80%931922%29). [İsmet Pasha (İnönü)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0smet_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC)'s organization of militia into a [regular army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Land_Forces) paid off when Ankara forces fought the Greeks in the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC) and [Second Battle of İnönü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC). The Greeks emerged victorious in the [Battle of Kütahya-Eskişehir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_K%C3%BCtahya%E2%80%93Eski%C5%9Fehir) and drove on Ankara. The Turks checked their advance in the [Battle of Sakarya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Sakarya) and counter-attacked in the [Great Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Offensive), which expelled Greek forces. The war ended with the [recapture of İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_capture_of_Smyrna), the [Chanak Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanak_Crisis) and another [armistice in Mudanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudanya).

The Grand National Assembly in Ankara was recognized as the legitimate Turkish government, which signed the [Treaty of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lausanne), a treaty more favorable to Turkey than Sèvres. The Allies evacuated Anatolia and eastern Thrace, the Ottoman government was overthrown, the monarchy abolished, and the [Grand National Assembly of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey) declared the Republic of Turkey on 29 October 1923. With the war, a [population exchange between Greece and Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_exchange_between_Greece_and_Turkey), the partitioning of the Ottoman Empire, and the abolition of the sultanate, the Ottoman era came to an end, and with Atatürk's reforms, the Turks created the secular nation of Turkey. Turkey's demographics were significantly affected by the [Armenian genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide) and the [Greek genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_genocide). The Turkish Nationalist Movement carried out massacres and deportations to eliminate native Christian populations—a continuation of the Armenian genocide and [other ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Ottoman_genocides) during World War I. The historic Christian presence in Anatolia was largely destroyed; Muslims went from 80% to 98% of the population.

## Background

See also: [Decline and modernization of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decline_and_modernization_of_the_Ottoman_Empire), [Dissolution of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Ottoman_Empire), [Committee of Union and Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress), and [Ottoman Empire during World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire_during_World_War_I)

![Front page of İkdam on 4 November 1918, "The Three Pashas Escaped"](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fc/%C4%B0kdam%2C_4_Kas%C4%B1m_1918.jpg)

*Front page of _[İkdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0kdam)_ on 4 November 1918, "The [Three Pashas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Pashas) Escaped"*

Following the chaotic politics of the [Second Constitutional Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Constitutional_Era), the Ottoman Empire came under the control of the [Committee of Union and Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress) in a [coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1913_Ottoman_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat) in 1913, and then further consolidated its control after the assassination of [Mahmud Shevket Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_Shevket_Pasha). Founded as a radical revolutionary group seeking to prevent a collapse of the Ottoman Empire, by the eve of World War I it decided that the solution was to implement nationalist and centralizing policies. The CUP reacted to the losses of land and the expulsion of Muslims from the [Balkan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Wars) by turning even more nationalistic. Part of its effort to consolidate power was to [proscribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proscription) and exile opposition politicians from the [Freedom and Accord Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_and_Accord_Party) to remote [Sinop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinop,_Turkey).

The [Unionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress) brought the Ottoman Empire into [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) on the side of [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) and [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), during which a genocidal campaign was waged against Ottoman Christians, namely [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide), [Pontic Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_genocide), and [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayfo). It was based on [an alleged conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causes_of_the_Armenian_genocide#Alleged_Armenian_conspiracy) that the three groups would rebel on the side of the Allies, so [collective punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_punishment) was applied. A similar suspicion and suppression from the Turkish nationalist government was directed towards the Arab and Kurdish populations, leading to localized rebellions. The [Entente powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entente_powers) reacted to these developments by charging the CUP leaders, commonly known as the [Three Pashas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Pashas), with "[crimes against humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_1915_Triple_Entente_declaration)" and threatened accountability. They also had imperialist ambitions on Ottoman territory, with correspondence over a post-war settlement in the Ottoman Empire being leaked to the press as the [Sykes–Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes%E2%80%93Picot_Agreement). Russia's [exit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brest-Litovsk) from World War I and descent into [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War) was driven in part by the Ottoman closure of the [Turkish straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_straits) to goods bound for [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). A new imperative was given to the Entente powers to knock the Ottoman Empire out of the war and restart the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_%28World_War_I%29).

World War I would be the nail in the coffin of [Ottomanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottomanism), an imperialist and multicultural nationalism. Mistreatment of non-Turk groups after 1913, and the general context of great socio-political upheaval that occurred in the [aftermath of World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_I), meant many minorities now wished to divorce their future from imperialism to form futures of their own by separating into (often [republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism)) [nation-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state). Due to the Turkish nationalist policies pursued by the CUP against Ottoman Christians by 1918 the Ottoman Empire held control over a mostly homogeneous land of Muslims from [eastern Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Thrace) to the Persian border. These included mostly [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people), as well as [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds), [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians), and [Muhacir groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhacir) from [Rumeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumelia). Most [Muslim Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Muslims) were now outside of the Ottoman Empire and under Allied occupation, with some "imperialists" still loyal to the Ottoman Sultanate-Caliphate, and others wishing for independence or Allied protection under a [League of Nations mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate). Sizable Greek and Armenian minorities remained within its borders, and most of these communities no longer wished to remain under the Empire.

### Conclusion of World War I

In the summer months of 1918, the leaders of the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers) realized that the [Great War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_War) was lost, including the Ottomans. Almost simultaneously, the [Palestinian Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_and_Palestine_campaign) and then the [Macedonian Front collapsed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_front). The sudden decision by [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bulgaria) to sign an [armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Salonica) cut communications from Constantinople ([İstanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul)) to [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna) and [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), and opened the undefended Ottoman capital to Entente attack. With the major fronts crumbling, Unionist [Grand Vizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ottoman_grand_viziers) [Talât Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talaat_Pasha) intended to sign an armistice, and resigned on 8 October 1918 so that a new government would receive less harsh armistice terms. The [Armistice of Mudros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudros) was signed on 30 October 1918, ending World War I for the Ottoman Empire. Three days later, the [Committee of Union and Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress) (CUP)—which governed the Ottoman Empire as a one-party state since [1913](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1913_Ottoman_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat)—held its last congress, where it was decided the party would be dissolved. Talât, [Enver Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enver_Pasha), [Cemal Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djemal_Pasha), [Doctor Nâzım](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Naz%C4%B1m), [Bahaeddin Şakir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahaeddin_%C5%9Eakir), and three other high-ranking members of the CUP escaped the Ottoman Empire on a German torpedo boat later that night, plunging the country into a power vacuum. With the fall of the CUP: the following factions hoped to take advantage of the power vacuum in the Ottoman Empire:

 * The Palace: With Sultan [Mehmed V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_V)'s death earlier in the summer of 1918, [Mehmed VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_VI) was girded with the [sword of Osman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sword_of_Osman). Unlike his half-brother, the new Sultan wished to reassert the monarchy as a center of power in the Ottoman Empire. In the following conflict, his singular goal was to safeguard the interests of the [royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_dynasty).
 * The Liberals: The [Freedom and Accord Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_and_Accord_Party) would be reestablished, and attempt to salvage the Ottoman Empire's diplomatic position by cooperating with Allied demands, though their detractors accused them of appeasement. Like its first period of operation from 1911–1913, Freedom and Accord Party again fell into infighting, and would be defunct by the summer of 1919. One of its old leaders, [Damat Ferid Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damat_Ferid_Pasha), would form a strong alliance with the Sultan, though he did not rejoin his party.
 * The [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I): [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) and [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) had several goals. Both countries hoped to carve up the Ottoman Empire with mandates and spheres of influence. Britain specifically focused on facilitating war crimes trials to try Ottoman war criminals. Their immediate short-term goal was to secure supply lines to assist the [Whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement) in the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War). Right after the armistice, a _de facto_ [Allied occupation began in Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_occupation_of_Constantinople). Some Ottoman intelligentsia hoped the Empire could become a mandate under the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), an upstart and trustworthy power that recently proclaimed the [Fourteen Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteen_Points).
 * Ethnic minorities: [Ottoman Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Greeks) hoped to join [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Greece) and [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_the_Ottoman_Empire) hoped to form a new [Armenian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia). [Pontic Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic_Greeks) hoped to establish their own state. In April 1919 they renounced their allegiance to the Ottoman state through their patriarchs. Some [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Kurds) hoped to establish an autonomous Kurdish state, but their claims overlapped with [Assyrian nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_nationalism).
 * Unionists: Though their leaders had escaped the country and the CUP as a whole was discredited and dissolved as an organization, ex-Unionists still controlled [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Chamber_of_Deputies), the [army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Army_%281861%E2%80%931922%29), police, post and telegraph, bureaucracy, and more. They were the target of purges which started by 1919, but the Allies and the Liberals did not have the resources or manpower to go after all of them. They would eventually coalesce around [Mustafa Kemal Pasha (Atatürk)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk).

## Prelude: October 1918 – May 1919

### Armistice of Mudros and occupation

![Allied occupation troops marching at the Grande Rue de Péra (İstiklal Avenue)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d3/Occupation_of_Constantinople_3.jpg)

*Allied occupation troops marching at the Grande Rue de Péra ([İstiklal Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0stiklal_Avenue))*

On 30 October 1918, the [Armistice of Mudros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudros) was signed between the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) and the [Allies of World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I), bringing hostilities in the [Middle Eastern theatre of World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern_theatre_of_World_War_I) to an end. The Ottoman Army was to demobilize, its [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Navy) and [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Airforce) handed to the Allies, and occupied territory in the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) and [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran) to be evacuated. Critically, Article VII granted the Allies the right to occupy forts controlling the [Turkish Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Straits) and the vague right to occupy "in case of disorder" any territory if there were a threat to security. The clause relating to the occupation of the straits was meant to secure a [Southern Russian intervention force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Russia_intervention), while the rest of the article was used to allow for Allied controlled [peace-keeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping) forces. There was also a hope to follow through punishing local actors that carried out exterminatory orders from the CUP government against [Armenian Ottomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_the_Ottoman_Empire). For now, the [House of Osman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_dynasty) escaped the fates of the [Hohenzollerns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenzollern), [Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg), and [Romanovs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Romanov) to continue ruling their empire, though at the cost of its remaining sovereignty.

The armistice was signed because the Ottoman Empire had been defeated in important fronts, but the military was intact and retreated in good order. Unlike other Central Powers, the Allies did not mandate an abdication of the [imperial family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_dynasty) as a condition for peace, nor did they request the [Ottoman Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Army_%281861%E2%80%931922%29) to dissolve its [general staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_General_Staff). Though the army suffered from mass [desertion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertion) throughout the war which led to [banditry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banditry), there was no threat of mutiny or revolutions like in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Revolution_of_1918%E2%80%931919), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_Austria-Hungary), or [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution). This is despite famine and economic collapse that was brought on by the extreme levels of [mobilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seferberlik), destruction from the war, [disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_flu), and mass murder since 1914.

On 13 November 1918, a French brigade entered Constantinople to begin a _de facto_ [occupation of the Ottoman capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Constantinople) and its immediate dependencies. This was followed by a fleet consisting of British, French, Italian and Greek ships deploying soldiers on the ground the next day, totaling 50,000 troops in Constantinople. The Allied Powers stated that the occupation was temporary and its purpose was to protect the [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_dynasty), the [caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Caliphate) and the [minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet_%28Ottoman_Empire%29). [Somerset Arthur Gough-Calthorpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerset_Arthur_Gough-Calthorpe)—the British signatory of the Mudros Armistice—stated the [Triple Entente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Entente)'s public position that they had no intention to dismantle the Ottoman government or place it under military occupation by "occupying Constantinople". However, dismantling the government and partitioning the Ottoman Empire among the Allied nations had been an objective of the Entente since the start of WWI.

A wave of seizures took place in the rest of the country in the following months. Questionably citing Article VII, the British occupied Mosul, claiming that Christian civilians in Mosul and [Zakho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakho) were killed en masse by the Turkish troops. In the Caucasus, Britain established a presence in [Menshevik Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menshevik_Georgia) and the [Lori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lori_Province) and [Aras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aras_%28river%29) valleys as [peace-keepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping). On 14 November, joint Franco-Greek occupation was established in the town of [Uzunköprü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzunk%C3%B6pr%C3%BC) in eastern Thrace as well as the railway axis until the train station of [Hadımköy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Had%C4%B1mk%C3%B6y_%28Metrobus%29) on the outskirts of Constantinople. On 1 December, British troops based in Syria occupied [Kilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilis), [Marash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahramanmara%C5%9F), [Urfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urfa) and [Birecik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birecik). Beginning in December, French troops began successive seizures of the [province of Adana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adana_vilayet), including the towns of [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antakya), [Mersin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersin), [Tarsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarsus_%28city%29), [Ceyhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceyhan), [Adana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adana), [Osmaniye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmaniye), and [İslâhiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0sl%C3%A2hiye), incorporating the area into the [Occupied Enemy Territory Administration North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupied_Enemy_Territory_Administration) while French forces embarked by [gunboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunboat) and sent troops to the [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea) ports of [Zonguldak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zonguldak) and [Karadeniz Ereğli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karadeniz_Ere%C4%9Fli), commanding Turkey's coal-mining region. These continued seizures of land prompted Ottoman commanders to refuse demobilization and prepare for the resumption of war.

### Prelude to resistance

![Mustafa Kemal Pasha in 1918, then an Ottoman army general](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1e/Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk_portrait.jpg)

*[Mustafa Kemal Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk) in 1918, then an Ottoman army general*

The British similarly asked [Mustafa Kemal Pasha (Atatürk)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk) to turn over the port of Alexandretta ([İskenderun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0skenderun)), which he reluctantly did, following which he was recalled to Constantinople. He made sure to distribute weapons to the population to prevent them from falling into the hands of Allied forces. Some of these weapons were smuggled to the east by members of [Karakol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakol_society), a successor to the CUP's [Special Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Organization_%28Ottoman_Empire%29), to be used in case resistance was necessary in Anatolia. Many Ottoman officials participated in efforts to conceal from the occupying authorities details of the burgeoning independence movement spreading throughout Anatolia.

Other commanders began refusing orders from the Ottoman government and the Allied powers. After Mustafa Kemal Pasha returned to Constantinople, [Ali Fuat Pasha (Cebesoy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Fuat_Cebesoy) brought [XX Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XX_Corps_%28Ottoman_Empire%29#After_Mudros) under his command. He marched first to [Konya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konya) and then to [Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara) to organise resistance groups, such as the [Circassian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians) [çetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87etes) he assembled with guerilla leader [Çerkes Ethem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87erkes_Ethem). Meanwhile, [Kâzım Karabekir Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A2z%C4%B1m_Karabekir) refused to surrender his intact and powerful [XV Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XV_Corps_%28Ottoman_Empire%29) in [Erzurum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzurum). Evacuating from the Caucusus, puppet republics and [Muslim militia groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_uprisings_in_Kars_and_Sharur%E2%80%93Nakhichevan) were established in the army's wake to hamper the consolidation of the new [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) state. Elsewhere in the country, regional nationalist resistance organizations known as _Şûrâs_ –meaning "councils", not unlike _[soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_%28council%29)_ in revolutionary Russia– were founded, most incorporating themselves into the [Defence of National Rights movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Defence_of_National_Rights) which protested continued Allied occupation and appeasement by the [Sublime Porte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sublime_Porte).

## The Armistice era

### Politics of de-Ittihadification

![Near East areas of control and front lines upon the Armistice of Mudros](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/25/Arabia_Armistice_Mudros.png)

*Near East areas of control and front lines upon the [Armistice of Mudros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudros)*

Following the occupation of Constantinople, [Mehmed VI Vahdettin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_VI) dissolved the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_%28Ottoman_Empire%29) which was dominated by Unionists [elected back in 1914](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1914_Ottoman_general_election), promising elections for the next year. Vahdettin just ascended to the throne only months earlier with the death of [Mehmed V Reşâd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_V_Re%C5%9Fad). He was disgusted with the policies of the CUP, and wished to be a more assertive sovereign than his diseased half brother. [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Greeks) and [Armenian Ottomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_the_Ottoman_Empire) declared the termination of their relationship with the Ottoman Empire through their respective [patriarchates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate), and refused to partake in any future election. With the collapse of the CUP and its censorship regime, an outpouring of condemnation against the party came from all parts of [Ottoman media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_the_Ottoman_Empire).

![Grand Vizier Ferid Pasha, damat of the royal family](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2d/Damad_Ferid_Pasha_1919.jpg)

*Grand Vizier [Ferid Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damat_Ferid_Pasha), [damat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damat) of the [royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_dynasty)*

A general amnesty was soon issued, allowing the exiled and imprisoned dissidents persecuted by the CUP to return to Constantinople. Sultan Vahdettin invited the pro-[Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_dynasty) politician [Damat Ferid Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damat_Ferid_Pasha) to form a government, whose members quickly set out to purge the [Unionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress) from the [Ottoman government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_late_Ottoman_Empire). Ferid Pasha hoped that his [Anglophilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglophile) and an attitude of [appeasement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeasement) would induce less harsh peace terms from the Allied powers. However, his appointment was problematic for the Unionists, many being members of the liquidated committee that were surely to face trial. Years of corruption, unconstitutional acts, war profiteering, and enrichment from ethnic cleansing and [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide) by the Unionists soon became basis of [war crimes trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecution_of_Ottoman_war_criminals) and [courts martial trials held in Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_trials_of_1919%E2%80%931920). While many leading Unionists were sentenced lengthy prison sentences, many made sure to escape the country before Allied occupation or to regions that the government now had minimal control over; thus most were sentenced _[in absentia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_in_absentia)_. The Allies encouragement of the proceedings and the use of British [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_exiles) as their holding ground made the trials unpopular. The partisan nature of the trials was not lost on observers either. The hanging of the [Kaymakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaymakam) of [Boğazlıyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bo%C4%9Fazl%C4%B1yan) district [Mehmed Kemal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmet_Kemal) resulted in a demonstration against the courts martials trials.

With all the chaotic politics in the capital and uncertainty of the severity of the incoming peace treaty, many Ottomans looked to Washington with the hope that the application of [Wilsonian principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteen_Points) would mean Constantinople would stay Turkish, as Muslims outnumbered Christians 2:1. The [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) never declared war on the Ottoman Empire, so many imperial elite believed Washington could be a neutral arbiter that could fix the empire's problems. [Halide Edip (Adıvar)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halide_Edib_Ad%C4%B1var) and her [Wilsonian Principles Society](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilson_Prensipleri_Cemiyeti) led the movement that advocated for the empire to be governed by an American [League of Nations Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate) (see [United States during the Turkish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_during_the_Turkish_War_of_Independence)). American diplomats attempted to ascertain a role they could play in the area with the [Harbord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbord_Commission) and [King–Crane Commissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King%E2%80%93Crane_Commission). However, with the collapse of [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson)'s health, the United States diplomatically withdrew from the Middle East to focus on Europe, leaving the Entente powers to construct a post-Ottoman [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_order).

### Banditry and the refugee crisis

![Muhacirs from the Balkan Wars waiting to cross the Bosphorus to Anatolia, Sirkeci, Istanbul, 1912](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/12/Balkan_G%C3%B6%C3%A7menleri_Sirkeci%27de_%281912%29.jpg)

*[Muhacirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhacir) from the [Balkan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Wars) waiting to cross the [Bosphorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosporus) to Anatolia, [Sirkeci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirkeci), [Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul), 1912*

The Entente would have arrived at Constantinople to discover an administration attempting to deal with decades of accumulated refugee crisis. The new government issued a proclamation allowing for deportees to [return to their homes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_return), but many Greeks and Armenians found their old homes occupied by desperate [Rumelian and Caucasian Muslim refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhacir) who were settled in their properties during the First World War. Ethnic conflict restarted in Anatolia; government officials responsible for resettling Christian refugees often assisted Muslim refugees in these disputes, prompting European powers to continue bringing Ottoman territory under their control. Of the 800,000 Ottoman Christian refugees, approximately over half returned to their homes by 1920. Meanwhile 1.4 million refugees from the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War) would pass through the Turkish straits and Anatolia, with 150,000 [White émigrés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_%C3%A9migr%C3%A9) choosing to settle in Istanbul for short or long term (see [Evacuation of the Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evacuation_of_the_Crimea)). Many provinces were simply depopulated from years of fighting, conscription, and ethnic cleansing (see [Ottoman casualties of World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_casualties_of_World_War_I)). The province of [Yozgat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yozgat) lost 50% of its Muslim population from conscription, while according to the governor of [Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van,_Turkey), almost 95% of its prewar residents were dead or [internally displaced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internally_displaced_person).

Administration in much of the Anatolian and Thracian countryside would soon all but collapse by 1919. Army deserters who turned to [banditry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banditry) essentially controlled fiefdoms with tacit approval from bureaucrats and local elites. An amnesty issued in late 1918 saw these bandits strengthen their positions and fight amongst each other instead of returning to civilian life. [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians_in_Turkey) and [Circassian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians_in_Turkey) [muhacirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhacir) resettled by the government in northwestern Anatolia and [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds_in_Turkey) in southeastern Anatolia were engaged in [blood feuds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_feuds) that intensified during the war and were hesitant to pledge allegiance to the Defence of Rights movement, and only would if officials could facilitate truces. Various Muhacir groups were suspicious of the continued [Unionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0ttihadism) ideology in the Defence of Rights movement, and the potential for themselves to meet fates 'like the Armenians' especially as warlords hailing from those communities assisted the deportations of the Christians even though as many commanders in the Nationalist movement also had Caucasian and Balkan Muslim ancestry.

### Mustafa Kemal's mission

![Sultan Mehmed VI after his sword girding](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/ba/Sultan_Mehmed_VI_of_the_Ottoman_Empire.jpg)

*Sultan [Mehmed VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_VI) after his sword girding*

With Anatolia in practical anarchy and the Ottoman army being questionably loyal in reaction to Allied land seizures, Mehmed VI established the military inspectorate system to reestablish authority over the remaining empire. Encouraged by Karabekir and [Edmund Allenby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Allenby,_1st_Viscount_Allenby), he assigned [Mustafa Kemal Pasha (Atatürk)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk) as the inspector of the [Ninth Army Troops Inspectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_Army_%28Ottoman_Empire%29#After_Mudros) –based in [Erzurum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzurum)– to restore order to Ottoman military units and to improve internal security on 30 April 1919, with his first assignment to suppress a rebellion by Greek rebels around the city of [Samsun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsun).

Mustafa Kemal was a well known, well respected, and well connected army commander, with much prestige coming from his status as the "Hero of [Anafartalar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Anfartalar)"—for his role in the [Gallipoli Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallipoli_Campaign)—and his title of "Honorary Aide-de-camp to His Majesty the Sultan" gained in the last months of WWI. This choice would seem curious, as he was a nationalist and a fierce critic of the government's accommodating policy to the Entente powers. He was also an early member of the CUP. However, Kemal Pasha did not associate himself with the fanatical faction of the CUP, many knew that he frequently clashed with the radicals of the Central Committee like Enver. He was therefore sidelined to the periphery of power throughout the Great War; after the CUP's dissolution he vocally aligned himself with moderates that formed the [Liberal People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Liberal_People%27s_Party) instead of the rump [Renewal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewal_Party_%28Ottoman_Empire%29) (both parties would be banned in May 1919 for being successors of the CUP). All these reasons allowed him to be the most legitimate nationalist for the sultan to placate. In this new political climate, Kemal, his friends, and soon to be sympathizers benefited from the purges, elevating to ever higher profile positions. He sought to capitalize on his war exploits to attain a better job, indeed several times he unsuccessfully lobbied for his inclusion in cabinet as [War Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_War_%28Ottoman_Empire%29). His new assignment gave him effective [plenipotentiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenipotentiary) powers over all of Anatolia which was meant to accommodate him and other nationalists to keep them loyal to the government.

Mustafa Kemal had earlier declined to become the leader of the [Sixth Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Army_%28Ottoman_Empire%29) headquartered in [Nusaybin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusaybin). But according to [Lord Kinross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Balfour,_3rd_Baron_Kinross), through manipulation and the help of friends and sympathizers, he became the inspector of virtually all of the Ottoman forces in Anatolia, tasked with overseeing the disbanding process of remaining Ottoman forces while at the same time suppressing a Greek uprising nearby [Samsun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsun). Kemal had an abundance of connections and personal friends concentrated in the post-armistice War Ministry, a powerful tool that would help him accomplish his secret goal: to lead a nationalist movement to safeguard Turkish interests against the Allied powers and a collaborative Ottoman government.

The day before his departure to [Samsun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsun) on the remote Black Sea coast, Kemal had one last audience with Sultan Vahdettin, where he affirmed his loyalty to the sultan-caliph. It was in this meeting that they were informed of the botched [occupation ceremony of Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_landing_at_Smyrna) (İzmir) by the Greeks. Kemal and his carefully selected staff left Constantinople aboard the old steamer [SS _Bandırma_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Band%C4%B1rma) on the evening of 16 May 1919.

### Negotiations for Ottoman partition

Main article: [Partition of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_the_Ottoman_Empire)

![Ahmed Tevfik Pasha (Okday) and Rıza Tevfik (Bölükbaşı) at the Paris Peace Conference](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/07/Img010GeneralBougrain.jpg)

*[Ahmed Tevfik Pasha (Okday)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmet_Tevfik_Pasha) and [Rıza Tevfik (Bölükbaşı)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%B1za_Tevfik_B%C3%B6l%C3%BCkba%C5%9F%C4%B1) at the [Paris Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference_%281919%E2%80%931920%29)*

On 19 January 1919, the [Paris Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference,_1919) was first held, at which Allied nations set the peace terms for the defeated [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers), including the Ottoman Empire. As a special body of the Paris Conference, "The Inter-Allied Commission on Mandates in Turkey", was established to pursue the secret treaties they had signed between 1915 and 1917. Italy sought control over the southern part of Anatolia under the [Agreement of St.-Jean-de-Maurienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreement_of_St.-Jean-de-Maurienne). France expected to exercise control over [Hatay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatay_Province), [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon), [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_%28region%29), and a portion of southeastern Anatolia based on the [Sykes–Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes%E2%80%93Picot_Agreement).

Greece justified their territorial claims of Ottoman land not least from Greece's entrance to WWI on the Allied side but also through the [Megali Idea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megali_Idea) as well as international sympathy from the suffering of [Ottoman Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Greeks) in [1914](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1914_Greek_deportations) and [1917–1918](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic_genocide). Privately, Greek prime minister [Eleftherios Venizelos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleftherios_Venizelos) had British prime minister [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George)'s backing because of his [Philhellenism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philhellenism), and from his charisma and charming personality. Greece's participation in the Allies' [Southern Russian intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Russia_intervention) also earned it favors in Paris. Venizelos' demands included parts of eastern Thrace, the islands of Imbros ([Gökçeada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6k%C3%A7eada)), Tenedos ([Bozcaada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozcaada)), and parts of Western Anatolia around the city of Smyrna ([İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmir)), all of which had large Greek populations. Venizelos also advocated a large Armenian state to check a post-war Ottoman Empire. Greece wanted to incorporate Constantinople, but Entente powers did not give permission. Damat Ferid Pasha went to Paris on behalf of the Ottoman Empire hoping to minimize territorial losses using [Fourteen Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteen_Points) rhetoric, wishing for a return to _[status quo ante bellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo_ante_bellum)_, on the basis that every province of the Empire held Muslim majorities. This plea was met with ridicule.

At the Paris Peace Conference, competing claims over [Western Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) by Greek and Italian delegations led Greece to land the flagship of the [Greek Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Navy) at Smyrna, resulting in the Italian delegation walking out of the peace talks. On 30 April, Italy responded to the possible idea of Greek incorporation of Western Anatolia by sending a warship to Smyrna as a show of force against the Greek campaign. A large Italian force [also landed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_occupation_of_Adalia) in [Antalya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antalya). Faced with Italian annexation of parts of Asia Minor with a significant ethnic Greek population, Venizelos secured Allied permission for Greek troops to land in Smyrna per Article VII, ostensibly as a [peacekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping) force to keep stability in the region. Venizelos's rhetoric was more directed against the CUP regime than the Turks as a whole, an attitude not always shared in the Greek military: "Greece is not making war against Islam, but against the anachronistic [[Unionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0ttihadism)] Government, and its corrupt, ignominious, and bloody administration, with a view to the expelling it from those territories where the majority of the population consists of Greeks." It was decided by the Triple Entente that Greece would control a zone around Smyrna and [Ayvalık](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayval%C4%B1k) in western Asia Minor.

## Organizational phase: May 1919 – March 1920

Main articles: [Turkish National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Movement) and [Kuva-yi Milliye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuva-yi_Milliye)

### Greek landing at Smyrna

Main article: [Greek landing at Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_landing_at_Smyrna)

![Greek troops marching on İzmir's coastal street, May 1919.](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a7/Greek_army_Smyrne_1919.jpg)

*Greek troops marching on [İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmir)'s coastal street, May 1919.*

Most historians mark the Greek landing at Smyrna on 15 May 1919 as the start date of the Turkish War of Independence as well as the start of the "[Kuva-yi Milliye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuva-yi_Milliye) Phase". The occupation ceremony from the outset was tense from nationalist fervor, with Ottoman Greeks greeting the soldiers with an ecstatic welcome, and Ottoman Muslims protesting the landing. A miscommunication in Greek high command led to an [Evzone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evzones) column marching by the municipal Turkish barracks. The nationalist journalist [Hasan Tahsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasan_Tahsin) fired the "first bullet" at the Greek standard bearer at the head of the troops, turning the city into a warzone. [Süleyman Fethi Bey](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BCleyman_Fethi_Bey) was murdered by bayonet for refusing to shout "Zito [Venizelos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleftherios_Venizelos)" (meaning "long live Venizelos, and 300–400 unarmed Turkish soldiers and civilians and 100 Greek soldiers and civilians were killed or wounded.

Greek troops moved from Smyrna outwards to towns on the [Karaburun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaburun_Peninsula,_Turkey) peninsula; to [Selçuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sel%C3%A7uk), situated a hundred kilometres south of the city at a key location that commands the fertile [Küçük Menderes River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BC%C3%A7%C3%BCk_Menderes_River) valley; and to [Menemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menemen) towards the north. [Guerilla warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla_warfare) commenced in the countryside, as Turks began to organize themselves into irregular guerilla groups known as [Kuva-yi Milliye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuva-yi_Milliye) (national forces), which were soon joined by Ottoman soldiers, bandits, and disaffected farmers. Most Kuva-yi Milliye bands were led by rogue military commanders and members of the [Special Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Organization_%28Ottoman_Empire%29). The Greek troops based in cosmopolitan Smyrna soon found themselves conducting counterinsurgency operations in a hostile, dominantly Muslim hinterland. Groups of Ottoman Greeks also formed contingents that cooperated with the [Greek Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Asia_Minor) to combat Kuva-yi Milliye within the zone of control. A [massacre of Turks at Menemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menemen_massacre) was followed up with a [battle for the town of Aydın](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ayd%C4%B1n), which saw intense intercommunal violence and the razing of the city. What was supposed to be a peacekeeping mission of Western Anatolia instead inflamed ethnic tensions and became a [counterinsurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterinsurgency).

![Sultanahmet demonstration, 25 May 1919](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/79/SultanahmetMitingi.jpg)

*[Sultanahmet demonstration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanahmet_demonstrations), 25 May 1919*

The reaction of Greek landing at Smyrna and continued Allied seizures of land served to destabilize Turkish civil society. Damat Ferid Pasha resigned as Grand Vizier, but the sultan reappointed him anyways. With the Chamber of Deputies dissolved, and the environment not looking conducive for an election, Sultan Mehmed VI called for a Sultanate Council (_Şûrâ-yı Saltanat_), so the government could be consulted by representatives of civil society how the Ottoman Empire should deal with its present predicaments. On 26 May 1919, 131 representatives of Ottoman civil society gathered in the capital as a faux parliament. Discussion focused on a new election for the Chamber of Deputies or to become a British or American mandate. By and large, the assembly was unsuccessful in its goals, and the Ottoman government did not develop a strategy to navigate the crises the empire was engulfed in.

Ottoman bureaucrats, military, and [bourgeoisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie) trusted the Allies to bring peace, and thought the terms offered at Mudros were considerably more lenient than they actually were. Pushback was potent in the capital, with 23 May 1919 being largest of the [Sultanahmet Square demonstrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanahmet_demonstrations) organized by the [Turkish Hearths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Hearths) against the Greek occupation of Smyrna, the largest act of [civil disobedience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_disobedience) in Turkish history at that point. The Ottoman government condemned the landing, but could do little about it.

### Organizing resistance

Mustafa Kemal Pasha and his colleagues stepped ashore in [Samsun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsun) on 19 May and set up their first quarters in the Mıntıka Palace Hotel. British troops were present in Samsun, and he initially maintained cordial contact. He had assured Damat Ferid about the army's loyalty towards the new government in Constantinople. However, behind the government's back, Kemal made the people of Samsun aware of the Greek and Italian landings, staged discreet mass meetings, made fast connections via telegraph with the army units in Anatolia, and began to form links with various Nationalist groups. He sent telegrams of protest to foreign embassies and the War Ministry about British reinforcements in the area and about British aid to Greek brigand gangs. After a week in Samsun, Kemal and his staff moved to [Havza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havza). It was there that he first showed the flag of the resistance.

[Kuva-yi Milliye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuva-yi_Milliye)

Mustafa Kemal wrote in his memoir that he needed nationwide support to justify armed resistance against the Allied occupation. His credentials and the importance of his position were not enough to inspire everyone. While officially occupied with the disarming of the army, he met with various contacts in order to build his movement's momentum. He met with [Rauf Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%BCseyin_Rauf_Orbay), Karabekir Pasha, [Ali Fuat Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Fuat_Cebesoy), and [Refet Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refet_Bele) and issued the [Amasya Circular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasya_Circular) (22 June 1919). Ottoman provincial authorities were notified via telegraph that the unity and independence of the nation was at risk, and that the government in Constantinople was compromised. To remedy this, a congress was to take place in Erzurum between delegates of the [Six Vilayets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_vilayets) to decide on a response, and another congress would take place in Sivas where every [Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilayet) should send delegates. Sympathy and a lack of coordination from the capital gave Mustafa Kemal freedom of movement and telegraph use despite his implied anti-government tone.

On 23 June, High Commissioner Admiral Calthorpe, realising the significance of Mustafa Kemal's discreet activities in Anatolia, sent a report about the Pasha to the [Foreign Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_and_Commonwealth_Office). His remarks were downplayed by George Kidson of the Eastern Department. Captain Hurst of the British occupation force in Samsun warned Admiral Calthorpe one more time, but Hurst's units were replaced with the [Brigade of Gurkhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade_of_Gurkhas). When the British landed in [Alexandretta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandretta), Admiral Calthorpe resigned on the basis that this was against the armistice that he had signed and was assigned to another position on 5 August 1919. The movement of British units alarmed the population of the region and convinced them that Mustafa Kemal was right.

### Consolidation through congresses

![Mustafa Kemal and his colleagues in Erzurum, 5 July 1919](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/87/Mustafa_Kemal_Pa%C5%9Fa%2C_Erzurum%2C_5_Temmuz_1919.png)

*Mustafa Kemal and his colleagues in [Erzurum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzurum_Congress), 5 July 1919*

By early July, Mustafa Kemal Pasha received telegrams from the sultan and Calthorpe, asking him and Refet to cease his activities in Anatolia and return to the capital. Kemal was in [Erzincan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzincan) and did not want to return to Constantinople, concerned that the foreign authorities might have designs for him beyond the sultan's plans. Before resigning from his position, he dispatched a circular to all nationalist organizations and military commanders to not disband or surrender unless for the latter if they could be replaced by cooperative nationalist commanders. Now only a civilian stripped of his command, Mustafa Kemal was at the mercy of the new inspector of [Third Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Army_%28Ottoman_Empire%29) (renamed from Ninth Army) Karabekir Pasha, indeed the War Ministry ordered him to arrest Kemal, an order which Karabekir refused. The [Erzurum Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzurum_Congress) was a meeting of delegates and governors from the six Eastern Vilayets. They drafted the [National Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misak-%C4%B1_Mill%C3%AE) (_Misak-ı Millî)_, which envisioned new borders for the Ottoman Empire by applying principles of [national self-determination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-determination) per [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson)'s [Fourteen Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteen_Points) and the abolition of [the capitulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitulations_of_the_Ottoman_Empire). The Erzurum Congress concluded with a circular that was effectively a declaration of independence: All regions within Ottoman borders upon the signing of the Mudros Armistice were indivisible from the Ottoman state –Greek and Armenian claims on Thrace and Anatolia were moot– and assistance from any country not coveting Ottoman territory was welcome. If the government in Constantinople was not able to attain this after electing a new parliament, they insisted a [provisional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_government) should be promulgated to defend Turkish sovereignty. The [Committee of Representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Representation) was established as a provisional executive body based in Anatolia, with Mustafa Kemal Pasha as its chairman.

![Borders and plebiscites of the National Pact outlined in the Erzurum Congress](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e7/Misak-i_milli.png)

*Borders and plebiscites of the [National Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Pact_%28Turkey%29) outlined in the [Erzurum Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzurum_Congress)*

Following the congress, the Committee of Representation relocated to Sivas. As announced in the Amasya Circular, a new congress was held there in September with delegates from all Anatolian and Thracian provinces. The [Sivas Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sivas_Congress) repeated the points of the National Pact agreed to in Erzurum, and united the various regional [Defence of National Rights Associations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Defence_of_National_Rights) organizations, into a united political organisation: [Anatolia and Rumeli Defence of Rights Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Defence_of_National_Rights) (A-RMHC), with Mustafa Kemal as its chairman. In an effort show his movement was in fact a new and unifying movement, the delegates had to swear an oath to discontinue their relations with the CUP and to never revive the party (despite most present in Sivas being previous members). It was also decided there that the Ottoman Empire should not be a League of Nations mandate under the United States, especially after the [U.S Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate) failed to ratify American membership in the League.

Momentum was now on the Nationalists' side. A [plot](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Galip_Olay%C4%B1) by a [loyalist Ottoman governor](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Galip_%28Elaz%C4%B1%C4%9F_valisi%29) and a [British intelligence officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Noel_%28Indian_Army_officer%29) to arrest Kemal before the Sivas Congress led to the cutting of all ties with the Ottoman government until a new election would be held in the lower house of parliament, the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_%28Ottoman_Empire%29). In October 1919, the last Ottoman governor loyal to Constantinople fled his province. Fearing the outbreak of hostilities, all British troops stationed in the Black Sea coast and [Kütahya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BCtahya) were evacuated. Damat Ferid Pasha resigned, and the sultan replaced him with a general with nationalist credentials: [Ali Rıza Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_R%C4%B1za_Pasha). On 16 October 1919, Ali Rıza and the Nationalists held negotiations in Amasya. They agreed in the [Amasya Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasya_Protocol) that an election would be called for the Ottoman Parliament to establish national unity by upholding the resolutions made in the Sivas Congress, including the National Pact.

By October 1919, the Ottoman government only held _de facto_ control over Constantinople; the rest of the Ottoman Empire was loyal to Kemal's movement to resist a partition of Anatolia and Thrace. Within a few months Mustafa Kemal went from General Inspector of the Ninth Army to a renegade military commander discharged for insubordination to leading a homegrown anti-Entente movement that overthrew a government and driven it into resistance.

### Last Ottoman parliament

![Fire caused by the British bombardment in Mudanya (6 July 1920)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3f/Mudanya_July_1920_%281%29.jpg)

*Fire caused by the British bombardment in [Mudanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudanya) (6 July 1920)*

In December 1919, an [election was held for the Ottoman parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919_Ottoman_general_election), with polls only open in unoccupied Anatolia and Thrace. It was boycotted by Ottoman Greeks, [Ottoman Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_the_Ottoman_Empire) and the [Freedom and Accord Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_and_Accord_Party), resulting in groups associated with the [Turkish Nationalist Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Movement) winning, including the A-RMHC. The Nationalists' obvious links to the CUP made the election especially polarizing and [voter intimidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_intimidation) and [ballot box stuffing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot_box_stuffing) in favor of the Kemalists were regular occurrences in rural provinces. This controversy led to many of the nationalist MPs organizing the [National Salvation Group](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fel%C3%A2h-%C4%B1_Vatan) separate from Kemal's movement, which risked the nationalist movement splitting in two.

Mustafa Kemal was elected an MP from Erzurum, but he expected the Allies neither to accept the [Harbord report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbord_Commission) nor to respect his parliamentary immunity if he went to the Ottoman capital, hence he remained in Anatolia. Mustafa Kemal and the Committee of Representation moved from Sivas to [Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara) so that he could keep in touch with as many deputies as possible as they traveled to Constantinople to attend the parliament.

Though Ali Rıza Pasha called the election as per the Amasya Protocol to keep unity between the "[Istanbul government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_Government)" and "[Ankara government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_government)", he was wrong to think the election could bring him any legitimacy. The Ottoman parliament was under the _de facto_ control of the British battalion stationed at Constantinople and any decisions by the parliament had to have the signatures of both Ali Rıza Pasha and the battalion's commanding officer. The only laws that passed were those acceptable to, or specifically ordered by the British. Nevertheless, the War Ministry established contact with National Forces fighting the Greeks, sending supplies, arms, and aide to militia.

On 12 January 1920, the last session of the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_%28Ottoman_Empire%29) met in the capital. First the sultan's speech was presented, and then a telegram from Mustafa Kemal, manifesting the claim that the rightful government of Turkey was in Ankara in the name of the Committee of Representation. On 28 January the MPs from both sides of the aisle secretly met to endorse the [National Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Pact_%28Turkey%29) as a peace settlement. They added to the points passed in Sivas, calling for plebiscites to be held in West Thrace; Batum, Kars, and Ardahan, and Arab lands on whether to stay in the Empire or not. Proposals were also made to elect Kemal president of the Chamber; however, this was deferred in the certain knowledge that the British would prorogue the Chamber. The Chamber of Deputies would be forcefully dissolved for passing the National Pact anyway. The National Pact solidified Nationalist interests, which were in conflict with the Allied plans.

From February to April, leaders of Britain, France, and Italy [met in London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_of_London_%281920%29) to discuss the partitioning of the Ottoman Empire and the crisis in Anatolia. The British began to sense that the elected Ottoman government was under Kemalist influence and if left unchecked, the Entente could once again find themselves at war with the Empire. The Ottoman government was not doing all that it could to suppress the Nationalists.

Mustafa Kemal manufactured a crisis to pressure the Istanbul government to pick a side by deploying Kuva-yi Milliye [towards İzmit](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Galip_Olay%C4%B1#Kuvâ-yi_Milliye%27nin_Güçlenmesi). The British, concerned about the security of the [Bosporus Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosporus), demanded Ali Rıza Pasha to reassert control over the area, to which he responded with his resignation to the sultan.

## Jurisdictional conflict: March 1920 – January 1921

### Decapitation of the Istanbul government

Main article: [Occupation of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Constantinople)

![British occupation troops marching in Istanbul's Pera (Beyoğlu) quarter](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ad/British_occupation_troops_marching_in_Beyoglu.jpg)

*British occupation troops marching in Istanbul's Pera ([Beyoğlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyo%C4%9Flu)) quarter*

As they were negotiating the [partition of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_the_Ottoman_Empire), the Allies were growing increasingly concerned about the Turkish National Movement. To this end, the Allied occupational authorities in Istanbul began to plan a raid to arrest nationalist politicians and journalists along with occupying military and police installations and government buildings. On 16 March 1920, the coup was carried out; several [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy) warships were anchored in the [Galata Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galata_Bridge) to support British forces, including the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army), while they carried out the arrests and occupied several government buildings in the early hours of the morning.

An Indian Army operation, the [Şehzadebaşı raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Eehzadeba%C5%9F%C4%B1_raid), resulted in 5 Ottoman soldiers from the [10th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Infantry_Division_%28Ottoman_Empire%29) being killed when troops raided their barracks. Among those arrested were the senior leadership of the Turkish National Movement and former members of the CUP. 150 arrested Turkish politicians accused of [war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crime) were interned in Malta and became known as the [Malta exiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_exiles).

Mustafa Kemal was ready for this move. He warned all the Nationalist organisations that there would be misleading declarations from the capital. He warned that the only way to counter Allied movements was to organise protests. He declared "Today the Turkish nation is called to defend its capacity for civilization, its right to life and independence – its entire future".

On 18 March, the Chamber of Deputies declared that it was unacceptable to arrest five of its members. On 11 April, the sultan Mehmed VI dissolve the parliament under pressure from the Allies, declaring the end of [Constitutional Monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Constitutional_Era) and a return to absolutism. University students were forbidden from joining political associations inside and outside the classroom. With the lower elected Chamber of Deputies shuttered, the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Ottoman_Empire) terminated, and the capital occupied; Sultan Vahdettin, his cabinet, and the appointed [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Ottoman_Empire) were all that remained of the Ottoman government, and were basically a puppet regime of the Allied powers. Grand Vizier [Salih Hulusi Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salih_Hulusi_Pasha) declared Mustafa Kemal's struggle legitimate, and resigned after less than a month in office. In his place, Damat Ferid Pasha returned to the premiership. The [Sublime Porte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sublime_Porte)'s decapitation by the Entente allowed Mustafa Kemal to consolidate his position as the sole leader of Turkish resistance against the Allies, and to that end made him the legitimate representative of the Turkish people.

### Promulgation of the Grand National Assembly

Main article: [Government of the Grand National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Grand_National_Assembly)

See also: [Grand National Assembly of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey)

![Opening of the Grand National Assembly](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a6/Opening_of_the_Grand_National_Assembly%2C_23_April_1920.jpg)

*Opening of the [Grand National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey)*

The strong measures taken against the Nationalists by the Allies in March 1920 began a distinct new phase of the conflict. Mustafa Kemal sent a note to the governors and force commanders, asking them to [conduct elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Turkish_Grand_National_Assembly_election) to provide delegates for a new parliament to represent the Ottoman (Turkish) people, which would convene in Ankara. With the proclamation of the counter-government, Kemal would then ask the sultan to accept its authority. Mustafa Kemal appealed to the [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world), asking for help to make sure that everyone knew he was still fighting in the name of the sultan who was also the caliph. He stated he wanted to free the caliph from the Allies. He found an ally in the [Khilafat movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khilafat_Movement) of [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India), where Indians protested Britain's planned dismemberment of Turkey. A committee was also started for sending funds to help the soon to be proclaimed Ankara government of Mustafa Kemal. A flood of supporters moved to Ankara just ahead of the Allied dragnets. Included among them were [Halide Edip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halide_Edib_Ad%C4%B1var) and [Abdülhak Adnan (Adıvar)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adnan_Ad%C4%B1var), [Mustafa İsmet Pasha (İnönü)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0smet_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC), [Mustafa Fevzi Pasha (Çakmak)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fevzi_%C3%87akmak), many of Kemal's allies in the Ministry of War, and [Celalettin Arif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celalettin_Arif), the president of the now shuttered Chamber of Deputies. Celaleddin Arif's desertion of the capital was of great significance, as he declared that the Ottoman Parliament had been dissolved illegally.

![Zones of control held by the Ankara government and the Allies. Istanbul contemptuously referred to anyone who supported the nationalist movement led by Mustafa Kemal as "Kemalî s" or "Kemalci s". Kemalî was used pejoratively as a reference to the Celalî rebels. The foreign press used the term "Kemalists" interchangeably with the word "nationalists" to denote the Ankara-based movement and its armed strength.](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9c/The_Angora_Government_and_its_Claims.jpg)

*Zones of control held by the [Ankara government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara_government) and the Allies. Istanbul contemptuously referred to anyone who supported the nationalist movement led by Mustafa Kemal as "_Kemalî_ s" or "_Kemalci_ s". _Kemalî_ was used pejoratively as a reference to the [Celalî rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celali_rebellions). The foreign press used the term "Kemalists" interchangeably with the word "nationalists" to denote the Ankara-based movement and its armed strength.*

Some 100 members of the Chamber of Deputies were able to escape the Allied roundup and joined 190 deputies elected. In March 1920, Turkish revolutionaries announced the establishment of a new parliament in Ankara known as the [Grand National Assembly of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey) (GNA) that was dominated by the A-RMHC. The [parliament](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meclis-i_Meb%C3%BBsan_6._d%C3%B6nem_mebuslar%C4%B1_listesi) included Turks, Circassians, Kurds, and one Jew. They met in a [building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Independence_Museum) that used to serve as the provincial headquarters of the local CUP chapter. The inclusion of "Turkey" in its name reflected an increasing trend of new ways Ottoman citizens thought of their country, and was the first time it was formally used as the name of the country. On 23 April, the assembly, assuming full governmental powers, gathered for the first time, electing Mustafa Kemal its first [Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Grand_National_Assembly) and [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ottoman_grand_viziers#Prime_Minister_of_the_Government_of_the_Grand_National_Assembly_%281920–1923%29).

Hoping to undermine the Nationalist Movement, Mehmed VI issued a [fatwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatwa) to qualify the Turkish revolutionaries as [infidels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infidel), calling for the death of its leaders. The fatwa stated that true believers should not go along with the Nationalist Movement as they committed [apostasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostasy). The [mufti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mufti) of Ankara [Rifat Börekçi](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmet_Rifat_B%C3%B6rek%C3%A7i) issued a simultaneous fatwa, declaring that the caliphate was under the control of the [Entente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) and the [Ferid Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damat_Ferid_Pasha) government. In this text, the Nationalist Movement's goal was stated as freeing the sultanate and the caliphate from its enemies. In reaction to the desertion of several prominent figures to the Nationalist Movement, Ferid Pasha ordered Halide Edip, [Ali Fuat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Fuat_Cebesoy) and Mustafa Kemal to be sentenced to death in absentia for treason.

### Clashes in İzmit

Main articles: [Revolts during the Turkish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolts_during_the_Turkish_War_of_Independence) and [Kuva-yi Inzibatiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuva-yi_Inzibatiye)

![A British officer inspecting Greek troops and trenches in Anatolia](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7f/British_officer_inspecting_Greek_troops_%28Anatolia_1919-1922%29.jpg)

*A British officer inspecting Greek troops and trenches in Anatolia*

The Istanbul government finally found an ally outside of the city walls in [Ahmet Anzavur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anzavur_Ahmed_Pasha). Throughout late 1919 and early 1920 the warlord recruited fellow Circassian bandits, decrying Kemal's nationalists as 'wicked Unionists and freemasons'.

On 28 April the sultan raised 4,000 soldiers known as the [Kuva-yi Inzibatiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuva-yi_Inzibatiye) (Caliphate Army) to combat the Nationalists. Then using money from the Allies, another force about 2,000 strong from non-Muslim inhabitants were initially deployed in [İznik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0znik). The sultan's government sent the forces under the name of the Caliphate Army to the revolutionaries to arouse counterrevolutionary sympathy. The British, being skeptical of how formidable these insurgents were, decided to use irregular power to counteract the revolutionaries. The Nationalist forces were distributed all around Turkey, so many smaller units were dispatched to face them. In İzmit there were two battalions of the British army. These units were to be used to rout the partisans under the command of Ali Fuat and Refet Pasha.

![Execution of a Kemalist by the British forces in Izmit (1920)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6b/Execution_of_a_Kemalist_Turk_in_Izmit_1920.jpg)

*Execution of a Kemalist by the British forces in Izmit (1920)*

Anatolia had many competing forces on its soil: British troops, Nationalist militia (Kuva-yi Milliye), the sultan's army (Kuva-yi Inzibatiye), and Anzavur's bands. On 13 April 1920, an uprising supported by Anzavur against the GNA occurred at Düzce as a direct consequence of the fatwa. Within days the rebellion spread to [Bolu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolu) and [Gerede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerede). The movement engulfed northwestern Anatolia for about a month. On 14 June, Nationalist militia fought a pitched battle near İzmit against the Kuva-yi İnzibatiye, Anzavur's bands, and British units. Under heavy attack some of the Kuva-yi İnzibatiye deserted and joined the Nationalist militia. Anzavur was not so lucky, as the Nationalists tasked [Ethem the Circassian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethem_the_Circassian) with crushing Anzavur's revolt. This revealed the sultan did not have the unwavering support of his own men and allies. Meanwhile, the rest of these forces withdrew behind the British lines which held their position. For now, Istanbul was out of Ankara's grasp.

The clash outside İzmit brought serious consequences. British troops attacked the Nationalists and the [Royal Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force) bombed their positions. This caused Nationalist forces to retreat temporarily to more secure locations. The British commander in Turkey, General [George Milne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Milne,_1st_Baron_Milne), asked for reinforcements. This led to a study to determine what would be required to defeat the Turkish Nationalists. The report, signed by French Field Marshal [Ferdinand Foch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Foch), concluded that 27 divisions were necessary, but the British army did not have 27 divisions to spare. Also, a deployment of this size could have disastrous political consequences back home. World War I had just ended, and the British public would not support another lengthy and costly expedition.

The British accepted the fact that a nationalist movement could not be defeated without deployment of well-trained forces. On 25 June, the Kuva-yi Inzibatiye was disbanded under British supervision. The British realised that the best option to overcome these Turkish Nationalists was to use a force that was battle-tested and fierce enough to fight the Turks on their own soil. The British had to look no further than Turkey's neighbor, already occupying part of its territory: Greece.

### Treaty of Sèvres

Main article: [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres)

![Borders (spheres of influence not shown) of the Ottoman Empire according to the unratified Treaty of Sèvres (1920) which was annulled and replaced by the Treaty of Lausanne in 1923](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/07/SevresTreaty.png)

*Borders (spheres of influence not shown) of the Ottoman Empire according to the unratified [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres) (1920) which was annulled and replaced by the [Treaty of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lausanne) in 1923*

Eleftherios Venizelos, pessimistic of the rapidly deteriorating situation in Anatolia, requested to the Allies that a peace treaty be drawn up with the hope that fighting would stop.

The subsequent [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres) in August 1920 confirmed the Arab [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilayet) of the empire would be reorganized into new nations given to Britain and France in the form of [Mandates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate) by the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations), while the rest of the Empire would be partitioned between Greece, Italy, [France (via Syrian mandate)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon), [Britain (via Iraqi mandate)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Iraq), [Armenia (potentially under an American mandate)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilsonian_Armenia), and [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Georgia). Smyrna would hold a plebiscite on whether to stay with Greece or Turkey, and the Kurdistan region would hold one on the question of independence. British, French, and Italian [spheres of influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere_of_influence) would also extend into Anatolia beyond the land concessions. The old capital of Constantinople as well as the Dardanelles would be under international League of Nations control.

However, the treaty could never come into effect. The treaty was extremely unpopular, with protests against the final document held even before its release in Sultanahmet square. Though Mehmed VI and Ferid Pasha loathed the treaty, they did not want Istanbul to join Ankara in nationalist struggle. The Ottoman government and Greece never ratified it. Though Ferid Pasha signed the treaty, the Ottoman Senate, the upper house with seats appointed by the sultan, refused to ratify the treaty. Greece disagreed on the borders drawn. The other allies began to fracture their support of the settlement immediately. Italy started openly supporting the Nationalists with arms by the end of 1920, and the French signed another [separate peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ankara_%281921%29) with Ankara only months later.

Kemal's GNA Government responded to the Treaty of Sèvres by promulgating [a new constitution in January 1921](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Constitution_of_1921). The resulting constitution consecrated the principle of popular sovereignty; authority not deriving from the unelected sultan, but from the Turkish people who elect governments representative of their interests. This document became the legal basis for the war of independence by the GNA, as the sultan's signature of the Treaty of Sèvres would be unconstitutional as his position was not elected. While the constitution did not specify a future role of the sultan, the document gave Kemal ever more legitimacy in the eyes of Turks for justified resistance against Istanbul.

## Fighting

### Southern Front

Main article: [Franco-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Turkish_War)

See also: [Franco-Syrian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Syrian_War), [Hananu Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hananu_Revolt), and [Alawite revolt of 1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alawite_revolt_of_1919)

![Military situation of Syria and Cilicia, January 1920](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bd/Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria.png)

*Military situation of Syria and Cilicia, January 1920*

![Turkish militias in Cilicia](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/da/Kuvva-i_Milliye_millitias%2C_1919.png)

*Turkish militias in Cilicia*

In contrast to the Eastern and Western fronts, it was mostly unorganized Kuva-yi Milliye which were fighting in the [Southern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Turkish_War) against France. They had help from the Syrians, who were fighting [their own war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Syrian_War) with the French.

The British troops which occupied coastal Syria by the end of World War I were [replaced by French troops over 1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1918_Anglo-French_Modus_Vivendi), with the Syrian interior going to [Faisal bin Al-Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_I_of_Iraq)'s self-proclaimed [Arab Kingdom of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria). France which wanted to take control of all of [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria) and [Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilicia). There was also a desire facilitate the return of Armenian refugees in the region to their homes, and the occupation force consisted of the [French Armenian Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Armenian_Legion) as well as various Armenian militia groups. 150,000 Armenians were repatriated to their homes within months of French occupation. On 21 January 1920, a Turkish Nationalist [uprising and siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Marash) occurred against the French garrison in [Marash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marash). The French position untenable they retreated to [Islahiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0slahiye), resulting in a massacre of many Armenians by Turkish militia. A grueling siege followed in [Antep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Aintab) which featured intense sectarian violence between Turks and Armenians. After a failed uprising by the Nationalists in Adana, by 1921, the French and Turks signed an [armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilicia_Peace_Treaty) and eventually a [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ankara_%281921%29) was brokered demarcating the border between the Ankara government and French controlled Syria. In the end, there was a mass exodus of Cilician Armenians to French controlled Syria, Previous Armenian survivors of deportation found themselves again as refugees and families which avoided the worst of the six years violence were forced from their homes, ending thousands of years of Christian presence in Southern Anatolia. With France being the first Allied power to recognize and negotiate with the Ankara government only months after signing the Treaty of Sèvres, it was the first to break from the coordinated Allied approach to the [Eastern question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_question). In 1923 the [Mandate for Syria and the Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon) under French authority would be proclaimed in former Ottoman territory.

Some efforts to coordinate between Turkish Nationalists and the Syrian rebels persisted from 1920 to 1921, with the Nationalists supporting the Faisal's kingdom through [Ibrahim Hanunu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Hananu) and [Alawite groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alawite_revolt_of_1919) which were also fighting the French. While the French conquered Syria, Cilicia had to be abandoned.

### Al-Jazira Front

Main articles: [United Kingdom during the Turkish War of Independence § Al Jazira front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_during_the_Turkish_War_of_Independence#Al_Jazira_front), and [Mahmud Barzanji revolts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_Barzanji_revolts)

![A photo which includes Ali İhsan Sâbis, Alexander Cobbe, General Robert Cassels, Gerard Leachman (who was killed in the 1920 Iraqi revolt) and other military personnel in front of Mosul Military Headquarters, 2 November 1918](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/65/2_November_1918_Northern_Iraq.png)

*A photo which includes [Ali İhsan Sâbis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_%C4%B0hsan_S%C3%A2bis), [Alexander Cobbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Cobbe), General [Robert Cassels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Cassels), [Gerard Leachman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Leachman) (who was killed in the [1920 Iraqi revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Iraqi_revolt)) and other military personnel in front of Mosul Military Headquarters, 2 November 1918*

Kuva-yi Milliye also engaged with British forces in the "[Al-Jazira Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_during_the_Turkish_War_of_Independence#Al_Jazira_front)," primarily in [Mosul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosul). [Ali İhsan Pasha (Sabis)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_%C4%B0hsan_Pasha) and his forces defending Mosul would surrender to the British in October 1918, but the British ignored the armistice and seized the city, following which the pasha also ignored the armistice and distributed weapons to the locals. Even before Mustafa Kemal's movement was fully organized, rogue commanders found allies in Kurdish tribes. The Kurds detested the taxes and centralization the British demanded, including [Shaykh Mahmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_Barzanji) of the [Barzani family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barzani_Family). Having previously supported the British invasion of Mesopotamia to become the governor of South Kurdistan, Mahmud revolted but was apprehended by 1919. Without legitimacy to govern the region, he was released from captivity to [Sulaymaniyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulaymaniyah), where he again declared an uprising against the British as the [King of Kurdistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kurdistan). Though an alliance existed with the Turks, little material support came to him from Ankara, and by 1923 there was a desire to cease hostilities between the Turks and British at Barzanji's expense. Mahmud was overthrown in 1924, and after a [1926 plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosul_question), Mosul was awarded to [British-controlled Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Iraq).

### Eastern Front

Main article: [Eastern Front (Turkey)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_%28Turkey%29)

See also: [Muslim uprisings in Kars and Sharur–Nakhichevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_uprisings_in_Kars_and_Sharur%E2%80%93Nakhichevan), [Turkish invasion of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_invasion_of_Armenia), and [Turkish invasion of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_invasion_of_Georgia)

See also: [Soviet Union–Turkey relations § Bolshevik support for Turkish revolutionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union%E2%80%93Turkey_relations#Bolshevik_support_for_Turkish_revolutionaries), and [Armenian–Azerbaijani war (1918–1920)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian%E2%80%93Azerbaijani_war_%281918%E2%80%931920%29)

![Fundraising poster by the American Committee for Relief in the Near East](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/99/Ethel_Franklin_Betts%2C_Save_the_Survivors%2C_1918_Cornell_CUL_PJM_2029_01.jpg)

*Fundraising poster by the [American Committee for Relief in the Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Committee_for_Relief_in_the_Near_East)*

Since 1917, the Caucasus was in a chaotic state. The border of newly independent [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) and the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) was defined in the [Treaty of Brest-Litovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brest-Litovsk) (3 March 1918) after the [Bolshevik revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution), and later by the [Treaty of Batum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Batum) (4 June 1918). To the east, Armenia was [at war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian%E2%80%93Azerbaijani_war_%281918%E2%80%931920%29) with the [Azerbaijan Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Democratic_Republic) after the breakup of the [Transcaucasian Democratic Federative Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasian_Democratic_Federative_Republic), and received support from [Anton Denikin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Denikin)'s [White Russian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_South_Russia). It was obvious that after the Armistice of Mudros (30 October 1918) the eastern border was not going to stay as it was drawn, which mandated the evacuation of the Ottoman army back to its 1914 borders. Right after the Armistice of Mudros was signed, pro-Ottoman provisional republics were proclaimed in [Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_National_Government_of_the_Southwestern_Caucasus) and [Aras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Aras) which were subsequently invaded by Armenia. Ottoman soldiers were convinced not to demobilize lest the area become a 'second [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_Struggle)'. Both sides of the new borders had massive refugee populations and famine, which were compounded by the renewed and more symmetric sectarian violence (see [massacres of Azerbaijanis in Armenia (1917–1921)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacres_of_Azerbaijanis_in_Armenia_%281917%E2%80%931921%29) and [Muslim uprisings in Kars and Sharur–Nakhichevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_uprisings_in_Kars_and_Sharur%E2%80%93Nakhichevan)). There were talks going on with the [Armenian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_diaspora) and Allied powers on reshaping the border. Woodrow Wilson agreed to transfer territories to Armenia based on the principles of national [self-determination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-determination). The results of these talks were to be reflected on the [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres) (10 August 1920).

[Kâzım Karabekir Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A2z%C4%B1m_Karabekir), commander of the [XV corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XV_Corps_%28Ottoman_Empire%29), encountered Muslim refugees fleeing from the Armenian army, but did not have the authority to cross the border. Karabekir's two reports (30 May and 4 June 1920) outlined the situation in the region. He recommended redrawing the eastern borders, especially around Erzurum. The Russian government was receptive to this and demanded that [Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van,_Turkey) and [Bitlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitlis) be transferred to Armenia. This was unacceptable to the Turkish revolutionaries. However, Soviet support was absolutely vital for the Turkish Nationalist movement, as Turkey was underdeveloped and had no domestic armaments industry. [Bakir Sami (Kunduh)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bekir_Sami_Kunduh) was assigned to negotiate with the Bolsheviks.

On 24 September 1920, Karabekir's [XV corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XV_Corps_%28Ottoman_Empire%29) and Kurdish militia [invaded Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_invasion_of_Armenia), advancing into [Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kars_%281920%29) and [Alexandropol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Alexandropol). With an advance on Yerevan imminent, on 28 November 1920, the [11th Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Army_%28RSFSR%29) under the command of [Anatoliy Gekker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoliy_Gekker) crossed over into Armenia from [Soviet Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Azerbaijan), and the Armenian government surrendered to Bolshevik forces.

The [Treaty of Alexandropol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Alexandropol) (2—3 December 1920) was the first treaty (although illegitimate) signed by the Turkish revolutionaries. The 10th article in the Treaty of Alexandropol stated that Armenia renounced the Treaty of Sèvres and its allotted partition of Anatolia. The agreement was signed with representatives of the former government of Armenia, which by that time had no _de jure_ or _de facto_ power in Armenia, since Soviet rule was already established in the country. On 16 March 1921, the Bolsheviks and Turkey signed a more comprehensive agreement, the [Treaty of Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kars), which involved representatives of [Soviet Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Armenia), [Soviet Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Azerbaijan), and [Soviet Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Georgia).

### Revolts

Main article: [Revolts during the Turkish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolts_during_the_Turkish_War_of_Independence)

### Western Front

Main article: [Greco-Turkish War (1919–1922)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_%281919%E2%80%931922%29)

![A photograph of Hellenic Army troops advancing on Nationalist positions during the 1920 Greek Summer Offensive](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/da/Ermoscharge.JPG)

*A photograph of [Hellenic Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Army) troops advancing on Nationalist positions during the 1920 [Greek Summer Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Summer_Offensive)*

The Greco-Turkish War—referred to as the "Western Front" by the Turks and the "Asia Minor Campaign" by the Greeks—started when Greek forces landed in [Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smyrna) (now İzmir), on 15 May 1919. A perimeter around the city known as the Milne Line was established in which low-intensity guerilla war commenced.

The conflict escalated when Greece and Britain performed a joint [offensive over the summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Summer_Offensive) of 1920, which Istanbul condemned, that took control over the Marmara coast and provided strategic depth to the İzmir occupation zone. The cities of İzmit, [Manisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manisa), [Balıkesir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bal%C4%B1kesir), [Aydın](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayd%C4%B1n), and [Bursa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursa) were taken with little Turkish resistance.

A second Greek offensive in autumn was launched with the goal to pressure Istanbul and Ankara to sign the Sèvres Treaty. This peace process was temporarily halted with the fall of Venizelos when the pro-Entente [King Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_of_Greece) died from sepsis after being bitten by a monkey. Much to Allied chagrin he was replaced by his anti-Entente father [King Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_I_of_Greece). Greece ceased to receive much Allied support after the change in power. The [Army of Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Asia_Minor) was purged of Venizelist officers, their replacements being less competent.

![Mustafa Kemal Pasha and his comrades-in-arms at the end of the First Battle of İnönü](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/01/Mustafa_Kemal_at_the_end_of_the_First_Battle_of_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC.png)

*Mustafa Kemal Pasha and his comrades-in-arms at the end of the [First Battle of İnönü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC)*

When the offensive resumed, the Turks received their first victory when the Greeks encountered stiff resistance in the battles of [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC) and [Second İnönü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC), due to İsmet Pasha's organization of an irregular militia into a regular army. The two victories led to Allied proposals to amend the Treaty of Sèvres where both Ankara and Istanbul were represented, but Greece refused. With the conclusion of the Southern and Eastern fronts, Ankara was able to concentrate more forces on the West against the Greeks. They also began to receive [support from Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union%E2%80%93Turkey_relations#Bolshevik_support_for_Turkish_revolutionaries), as well as Italy and France, who sought to check British influence in the Near East.

According to Soviet documents, Soviet financial and war material support between 1920 and 1922 amounted to: 39,000 rifles, 327 machine guns, 54 cannon, 63 million rifle bullets, 147,000 shells, 2 patrol boats, 200.6 kg of gold ingots and 10.7 million [Turkish lira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_lira) (which accounted for a twentieth of the Turkish budget during the war). Additionally the Soviets gave the Turkish nationalists 100,000 [gold rubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_ruble) to help build an orphanage and 20,000 lira to obtain printing house equipment and cinema equipment.

June–July 1921 saw heavy fighting in the [Battle of Kütahya-Eskişehir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_K%C3%BCtahya%E2%80%93Eski%C5%9Fehir). While it was an eventual Greek victory, the Turkish army withdrew in good order to the [Sakarya river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakarya_River), their last line of defence. Mustafa Kemal Pasha [replaced](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C5%9Fkumandanl%C4%B1k_Kanunu) İsmet Pasha after the defeat as commander-in-chief as well as his political duties. The decision was made in the Greek military command to march on the Nationalist capital of Ankara to force Mustafa Kemal to the negotiating table. For 21 days, the Turks and Greeks fought a pitched [battle at the Sakarya river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Sakarya), which ended in Greek withdrawal. Almost of year of stalemate without much fighting followed, during which Greek morale and discipline faltered while Turkish strength increased. French and Italian forces evacuated Anatolia. The Allies offered an armistice to the Turks, which Mustafa Kemal refused.

#### Peace negotiations and the Great Offensive (1921–1922)

Further information: [Conference of London of 1921–1922](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_of_London_of_1921%E2%80%931922) and [Chanak Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanak_Crisis)

![A political cartoon: Greek king Constantine runs away from the bomb which reads "KEMAL"](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3d/Turkije_verjaagt_Griekenland_In_Klein-Azi%C3%AB_%28titel_op_object%29%2C_NG-505-108.jpg)

*A political cartoon: Greek king Constantine runs away from the bomb which reads "KEMAL"*

In salvaging the Treaty of Sèvres, The Triple Entente forced the Turkish revolutionaries to agree with the terms through a series of conferences in London. The conference of London gave the Triple Entente an opportunity to reverse some of its policies. In October, parties to the conference received a report from Admiral [Mark Lambert Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Lambert_Bristol). He organised a commission to analyse the situation, and inquire into the bloodshed during the Occupation of İzmir and the following activities in the region. The commission reported that if annexation would not follow, Greece should not be the only occupation force in this area. Admiral Bristol was not so sure how to explain this annexation to [U.S. President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) Woodrow Wilson as he insisted on "respect for nationalities" in the Fourteen Points. He believed that the sentiments of the Turks "will never accept this annexation".

Neither the Conference of London nor Admiral Mark Lambert Bristol's report changed British prime minister [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George)'s position. On 12 February 1921, he went with the annexation of the Aegean coast which was followed by the Greek offensive. David Lloyd George acted with his sentiments, which were developed during [Battle of Gallipoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallipoli_campaign), as opposed to [General Milne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Milne,_1st_Baron_Milne), who was his officer on the ground.

![Turkish troops enter Constantinople on 6 October 1923](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f9/Liberation_of_Istanbul_on_October_6%2C_1923.jpg)

*Turkish troops enter Constantinople on 6 October 1923*

First negotiations between the sides failed during the Conference of London. The stage for peace was set after the Triple Entente's decision to make an arrangement with the Turkish revolutionaries. Before the talks with the Entente, the Nationalists partially settled their eastern borders with the Democratic Republic of Armenia, signing the [Treaty of Alexandropol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Alexandropol), but changes in the Caucasus—especially the establishment of the [Armenian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic)—required one more round of talks. The outcome was the [Treaty of Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kars), a successor treaty to the earlier [Treaty of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Moscow_%281921%29) of March 1921. It was signed in [Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kars) with the [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic) on 13 October 1921 and ratified in [Yerevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerevan) on 11 September 1922.

With the borders secured with treaties and agreements at east and south, Mustafa Kemal was now in a commanding position. On August 26, 1922, in the [Battle of Dumlupınar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dumlup%C4%B1nar), the Turks routed the Greek positions and launched the [Great Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Offensive). The Nationalists demanded that the Greek army evacuate East Thrace, Imbros, and Tenedos as well as Asia Minor. Mustafa Kemal sent a telegram to his commanders: "Armies! Your first goal is the Mediterranean, onwards!" The Turks recaptured all of Greece's gains in the span of three weeks, and resulted in the [recapture of Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_capture_of_Smyrna) by Turkish forces right after which occurred the [great fire of Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_fire_of_Smyrna). Greece's retreat from Anatolia saw its army committing scorched earth tactics and the depopulation of Muslim villages.

The British were prepared to defend the neutral zone of [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) and the Straits and the French asked Kemal to respect it, to which he agreed on 28 September. However, France, Italy, Yugoslavia, and the British Dominions objected to a new war. France, Italy and Britain called on Mustafa Kemal to enter into cease-fire negotiations. In return, on 29 September Kemal asked for the negotiations to be started at [Mudanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudanya). This was agreed on 11 October, two [hours before the British intended to engage Nationalist forces at Çanak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanak_Crisis), and signed the next day. The Greeks initially refused to agree but did so on 13 October. Factors persuading Turkey to sign may have included the arrival of British reinforcements. With the British government and public firmly anti-war, the [Chanak Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanak_Crisis) led to the collapse of David Lloyd George's coalition government.

#### Armistice of Mudanya

Further information: [Armistice of Mudanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudanya)

The Marmara sea resort town of [Mudanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudanya) hosted the conference to arrange the armistice on 3 October 1922. İsmet Pasha—commander of the western armies—was in front of the Allies. The scene was unlike Mudros as the British and the Greeks were on the defence. Greece was represented by the Allies.

The British still expected the GNA to make concessions. From the first speech, the British were startled as Ankara demanded fulfillment of the National Pact. During the conference, the British troops in Constantinople were preparing for a Kemalist attack. There was never any fighting in Thrace, as Greek units withdrew before the Turks crossed the straits from Asia Minor. The only concession that İsmet made to the British was an agreement that his troops would not advance any farther toward the Dardanelles, which gave a safe haven for the British troops as long as the conference continued. The conference dragged on far beyond the original expectations. In the end, it was the British who yielded to Ankara's advances.

![Kemal Pasha inspects the Turkish troops (18 June 1922)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d1/Atat%C3%BCrk_asker%C3%AE_birlikleri_denetlerken%2C_%C4%B0zmit%2C_18_Haziran_1922.png)

*Kemal Pasha inspects the Turkish troops (18 June 1922)*

The [Armistice of Mudanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudanya) was signed on 11 October. By its terms, the Greek army would move west of the [Maritsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritsa), clearing eastern Thrace to the Allies. The famous American author [Ernest Hemingway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Hemingway) was in Thrace at the time, and he covered the evacuation of eastern Thrace of its Greek population. He has several short stories written about Thrace and Smyrna, which appear in his book _[In Our Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Our_Time_%28short_story_collection%29)_. The agreement came into force starting 15 October. Allied forces would stay in eastern Thrace for a month to assure law and order. In return, Ankara would recognise continued British occupation of Constantinople and the Straits zones until the final treaty was signed.

Refet Bele was assigned to seize control of eastern Thrace from the Allies. He was the first representative to reach the old capital. The British did not allow the hundred gendarmes who came with him. That resistance lasted until the next day.

## Outcome

### Abolition of the sultanate

Kemal had long ago made up his mind to abolish the sultanate when the moment was ripe. After facing opposition from some members of the assembly, using his influence as a war hero, he managed to prepare a draft law for the abolition of the sultanate, which was then submitted to the National Assembly for voting. In that article, it was stated that the form of the government in Constantinople, resting on the sovereignty of an individual, had already ceased to exist when the British forces occupied the city after World War I. Furthermore, it was argued that although the [caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate) had belonged to the Ottoman Empire, it rested on the Turkish state by its dissolution and Turkish National Assembly would have right to choose a member of the Ottoman family in the office of caliph. On 1 November, The Turkish Grand National Assembly voted to [abolish the sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_Ottoman_sultanate). Mehmed VI fled Turkey on 17 November 1922 on [HMS Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Malaya); so ended [the over 600 year-old monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Ottoman_Empire). [Ahmed Tevfik Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmet_Tevfik_Pasha) also resigned as Grand Vizier (Prime Minister) a couple days later, without a replacement.

### Treaty of Lausanne

![The Treaty of Lausanne, signed in 1923 that guaranteed Turkey's independence, replacing the Treaty of Sèvres](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/58/Turkey-Greece-Bulgaria_on_Treaty_of_Lausanne.png)

*The Treaty of Lausanne, signed in 1923 that guaranteed Turkey's independence, replacing the Treaty of Sèvres*

The [Conference of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne_Conference_of_1922%E2%80%9323) began on 21 November 1922 in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne), Switzerland and lasted into 1923. Its purpose was the negotiation of a treaty to replace the [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres), which, under the new government of the Grand National Assembly, was no longer recognised by [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey). [İsmet Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0smet_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC) was the leading Turkish negotiator. İsmet maintained the basic position of the Ankara government that it had to be treated as an independent and sovereign state, equal with all other states attending the conference. In accordance with the directives of Mustafa Kemal, while discussing matters regarding the control of Turkish finances and justice, the [Capitulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitulations_of_the_Ottoman_Empire), the [Turkish Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Straits) and the like, he refused any proposal that would compromise Turkish sovereignty. Finally, after long debates, on 24 July 1923, the [Treaty of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lausanne_%281923%29) was signed. Ten weeks after the signature the Allied forces left Istanbul.

The conference opened with representatives from the United Kingdom, France, Italy and Turkey. It heard speeches from [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini) of Italy and [Raymond Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Poincar%C3%A9) of France. At its conclusion, Turkey assented to the political clauses and the "freedom of the [straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dardanelles)", which was Britain's main concern. The matter of [the status of Mosul was deferred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosul_question), since [Curzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Curzon,_1st_Marquess_Curzon_of_Kedleston) refused to be budged on the British position that the area was part of Iraq. The British Iraq Mandate's possession of Mosul was confirmed by a League of Nations brokered agreement between Turkey and Great Britain in 1926. The French delegation, however, did not achieve any of their goals and on 30 January 1923 issued a statement that they did not consider the draft treaty to be any more than a "basis of discussion". The Turks therefore refused to sign the treaty. On 4 February 1923, Curzon made a final appeal to İsmet Pasha to sign, and when he refused the Foreign Secretary broke off negotiations and left that night on the [Orient Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient_Express).

The [Treaty of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lausanne), finally signed in July 1923, led to international recognition of the Grand National Assembly as the legitimate government of Turkey and sovereignty of the [Republic of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Turkey) as the [successor state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Successor_state) to the defunct [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire). Most goals on the condition of sovereignty were granted to Turkey. In addition to Turkey's more favourable land borders compared with Treaty of Sèvres (as can be seen in the picture to the right), capitulations were abolished, the issue of Mosul would be decided by a League of Nations [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite) in 1926, while the border with Greece and Bulgaria would become demilitarised. The Turkish Straits would be under an international commission which gave Turkey more of a voice (this arrangement would be replaced by the [Montreux Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreux_Convention_Regarding_the_Regime_of_the_Straits) in 1936). The [Maritsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritsa) (Meriç) River would again become the western border of Turkey, as it was before 1914.

### Establishment of the Republic

Turkey was [proclaimed a Republic on 29 October 1923](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Day_%28Turkey%29), with Mustafa Kemal Pasha was elected as the first President. In forming his government, he placed [Mustafa Fevzi (Çakmak)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fevzi_%C3%87akmak), [Köprülü Kâzım (Özalp)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A2z%C4%B1m_%C3%96zalp), and [İsmet (İnönü)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0smet_%C4%B0n%C3%B6n%C3%BC) in important positions. They helped him to establish his [subsequent political and social reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atat%C3%BCrk%27s_Reforms) in Turkey, transforming the country into a modern and secular nation state.

## Historiography

Main article: [Kemalist historiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemalist_historiography)

The orthodox Turkish perspective on the war is based primarily on the speeches (see [Nutuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutuk)) and narratives of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, a high-ranking officer in World War I and the leader of the Nationalist Movement. Kemal was characterized as the founder and sole leader of the Nationalist Movement. Potentially negative facts were omitted in the orthodox historiography. This interpretation had a tremendous influence on the perception of Turkish history, even by foreign researchers. The more recent historiography has come to understand the Kemalist version as a nationalist framing of events and movements leading to the republic's founding. This was accomplished by sidelining unwanted elements which had links to the detested and genocidal CUP, and thus elevating Kemal and his policies.: 805–806

![Propaganda poster of the Turkish National Movement](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4d/Hal%C3%A2sk%C3%A2r%C3%A2n-%C4%B1_%C4%B0sl%C3%A2m_afi%C5%9F.jpg)

*Propaganda poster of the [Turkish National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Movement)*

In the orthodox Turkish version of events, the Nationalist Movement broke with its defective past and took its strength from popular support led by Kemal, consequently giving him the surname _Atatürk_, meaning "Father of Turks". According to historians such as [Donald Bloxham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Bloxham), [E.J. Zürcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik-Jan_Z%C3%BCrcher), and [Taner Akçam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taner_Ak%C3%A7am), this was not the case in reality, and a nationalist movement emerged through the backing of leaders of CUP, of whom many were war criminals, people who became wealthy with confiscated equities and they were not on trial for their crimes owing to the accelerating support for the National Movement. Kemalist figures, including many old members of the CUP, ended up writing the majority of the history of the war. The modern understanding in Turkey is greatly influenced by this nationalist and politically motivated history.: 806

The claim that the Nationalist Movement emerged as a continuation of the CUP is based on the fact Nationalist leaders such as: [Kâzım Karabekir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A2z%C4%B1m_Karabekir) and [Fethi Okyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fethi_Okyar) had been former members of the committee. However, their conduct during and after the war shows that various movements were competing with each other. Kazım Karabekir had [Halil Kut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halil_Kut) (Enver Pasha's uncle) deported from Anatolia during the war. Suspecting that he may reorganize the CUP through [Enver Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enver_Pasha)'s directives, Mustafa Kemal appointed [Ali Fuat Cebesoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Fuat_Cebesoy) as a representative to Moscow after learning Enver Pasha was lobbying in the [RSFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic) as he made promises to return Anatolia during [Baku Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Congress). In July 1921 Enver Pasha organized a congress in [Batumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batumi) for former CUP members who were now [Grand National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey) deputies. They intended to seize power and expected the [Kemalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Movement) would lose the [Battle of the Sakarya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Sakarya). Due to Enver's leadership of the [Basmachi movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basmachi_movement) and Djemal's visit to [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan), [Fahri Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakhri_Pasha) was appointed ambassador to Afghanistan to minimize their efforts; Turkey and Afghanistan signed a friendship treaty. After the war former high-ranking CUP members were semi-active in politics until they were purged following an [alleged assassination attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmir_plot) on Mustafa Kemal's life. Former Finance minister [Mehmed Cavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_Cavid) and Politician [Ziya Hurşit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziya_Hur%C5%9Fit) were found guilty and executed and former members like Kâzım Karabekir were put on trial but acquitted

According to Mesut Uyar, the Turkish War of Independence was also a [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_war) which took place in [Southern Marmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmara_Region), [Western and Eastern Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Region), and [Central Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Anatolia_Region) regions. He states that its aspect as a civil war is pushed into the background in official and academic books as 'revolts'. The losers of civil war who neither supported sultan nor Ankara Government, which they considered a continuation of CUP, did not consider themselves rebels. He further emphasizes that casualties and financial losses that occurred in the civil war is at least as catastrophic as the war that was fought against the enemies in other fronts. Thus, he concludes that the war was similar to the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution).

![The Abilene Daily Reporter based](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/16/The_Abilene_Daily_Reporter_%28Abilene%2C_Tex.%29%2C_Vol._24%2C_No._133%2C_Ed._1_Friday%2C_October_13%2C_1922_-_DPLA_-_4f8cde42a03b2ac9f5ff0aa395b4babf_%28page_1%29_%28cropped%29.jpg)

*_[The Abilene Daily Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Abilene_Daily_Reporter)_ based in [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas), U.S., called Mustafa Kemal "Turkey's [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington)" on 13 October 1922*

Preference of the term "Kurtuluş Savaşı" (lit. Liberation War) has been criticized by Corry Guttstadt as it causes Turkey to be portrayed as "a victim of imperialist forces". In this version of events, minority groups are depicted as a pawn used by these forces. Turkish Islamists, right-wing faction and also leftists regard this historical narrative to be legitimate. In fact, Ottoman Empire had joined the First World War with expansionist goals. The CUP government intended to expand the Empire into [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia). When they were defeated, however, they depicted themselves as the victims, even though war brought dire consequences for non-Muslim minorities. Guttstadt states that Turkish War of Independence, which was conducted against Armenian and Greek minorities, was an Islamist campaign as National Defense Committees were organizations founded with Islamist characteristics. On the other hand, the embrace of the Turkish War of Independence by Islamists is not common. During the war, Islamists such as Ottoman [Shaykh al-Islām](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Sheikh-ul-Islams_of_the_Ottoman_Empire) [Mustafa Sabri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Sabri) accused the Ankara-based Nationalist Movement of being a rebellion against the caliphate and the monarchy. After the war, Islamists, disturbed by Mustafa Kemal's secularist [reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atat%C3%BCrk%27s_reforms) in Republican Turkey, put forward various [conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theories_in_Turkey#List_of_conspiracy_theories) to try to discredit both the war and Kemal, the commander-in-chief of the Turkish side.

However, from the Turkish perspective, the term "Kurtuluş Savaşı" is widely defended, as the overwhelming majority of Turks view the event as a liberation from a foreign occupation. A speech delivered by Mustafa Kemal on 24 April 1920, to the newly established Ankara government, summed up the Turkish perspective of the situation: "It is known to all that the seat of the [Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Caliphate) and the [Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Assembly_of_the_Ottoman_Empire) is under temporary occupation by foreign forces and that our independence is greatly restricted. Submitting to these conditions would mean national acceptance of a slavery proposed to us by foreign powers." The Treaty of Sèvres further promoted the Turkish narrative of the need to "liberate" the country. Should no action be taken, the Turkish state would be reduced to rump state in central Anatolia under heavy foreign influence.

Armenian historian [Richard G. Hovannisian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_G._Hovannisian) writes that the Italians were "currying favor" with Turkish Nationalist forces by allowing "clandestine sale and shipment of arms" to them.

## Consequences

### Ethnic cleansing

Main articles: [Armenian genocide § Turkish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide#Turkish_War_of_Independence), [Greek genocide § Greco-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_genocide#Greco-Turkish_War), and [Greco-Turkish War (1919–1922) § Greek massacres of Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_%281919%E2%80%931922%29#Greek_massacres_of_Turks)

Further information: [Turkish war crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_war_crimes)

See also: [Late Ottoman genocides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Ottoman_genocides) and [Population exchange between Greece and Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_exchange_between_Greece_and_Turkey)

Historian Erik Sjöberg concludes that "It seems, in the end, unlikely that the Turkish Nationalist leaders, though secular in name, ever had any intention of allowing any sizeable non-Muslim minority to remain." According to [Rıza Nur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%B1za_Nur), one of the Turkish delegates at Lausanne, wrote that "disposing of people of different races, languages and religions in our country is the most... vital issue". Many Greek men were conscripted into unarmed [labor battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Battalions_%28Ottoman_Empire%29) where the death rate sometimes exceeded 90 percent. [Raymond Kévorkian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_K%C3%A9vorkian) states that "removing non-Turks from the sanctuary of Anatolia continued to be one of" the Turkish Nationalists' main activities after World War I. Preventing Armenians and other Christians from returning home, and therefore allowing their properties to be retained by those who had stolen them during the war, was a key factor in securing popular support for the Turkish Nationalist Movement. Christian civilians were subjected to forced deportation to expel them from the country, a policy that continued after the war. These deportations were similar to those employed during the Armenian Genocide and caused many deaths. Over 1 million Greeks were expelled as were all remaining Armenians in the areas of [Diyarbekir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyarbak%C4%B1r), [Mardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardin), [Urfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urfa), [Harput](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpoot), and [Malatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malatya)—forced across the border into French-mandate Syria. Greek and Armenian civilians were massacred in the [fire of Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_of_Smyrna) and later during the [Turkish invasion of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_invasion_of_Armenia).

Vahagn Avedian argues that the Turkish War of Independence was not directed against the Allied Powers, but that its main objective was to get rid of non-Turkish minority groups. The Nationalist movement maintained the aggressive policy of the CUP against Christians. It was stated in a secret telegram from Foreign Minister [Ahmet Muhtar (Mollaoğlu)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmet_Muhtar_Mollao%C4%9Flu) to Kazım Karabekir in mid-1921 "the most important thing is to eliminate Armenia, both politically and materially". Avedian holds that the existence of the [Armenian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Republic_%28disambiguation%29) was considered as the "greatest threat" for the continuation of Turkish state, and that for this reason, they "fulfilled the genocidal policy of its CUP predecessor". After the Christian population was destroyed, the focus shifted to the [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_population) population. Ethnic cleansing was also carried against [Pontic Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic_Greeks) with the collaboration with Ankara and Istanbul governments.

### Turkey

![Hatıra-i Zafer (Memory of Victory) by Hasan Sabri in 1925.](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/36/Memory_of_Victory%2C_1925.jpg)

*_Hatıra-i Zafer_ (Memory of Victory) by Hasan Sabri in 1925.*

The [Grand National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Turkey) transitioned from a provisional counsel to being Turkey's primary legislative body. In 1923, A-RMHC changed its name to the People's Party. A couple years later, the name would be changed again by Mustafa Kemal to the [Republican People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_People%27s_Party) (Cumhuriyet Halk Partisi, CHP), one of Turkey's major political parties as well as its oldest. CHP went on to rule [Turkey as a one party state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-party_period_of_the_Republic_of_Turkey) until the [1946 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_Turkish_general_election).

### Aftermath of the Chanak Crisis

In addition to toppling the British government, the [Chanak Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanak_Crisis) would have far reaching consequences on British dominion policy. As the Dominion of [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada) did not see itself committed to support a potential British war with Kemal's GNA, dominion foreign policy would become less committed for security for the British Empire. This attitude of no commitment to the Empire would be a defining moment in Canada's gradual movement towards independence as well as the decline of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire).

### Influence on other nations

The media in [Weimar Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic) covered the events in Anatolia extensively. Ihrig argues that Turkish War of Independence had a more definite effect on the [Beer Hall Putsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_Hall_Putsch) than Mussolini's [March on Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_on_Rome). Germans, including [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler), wanted to abolish the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles) just like the Treaty of Sèvres was abolished. After the failed putsch media coverage on the war ceased.
