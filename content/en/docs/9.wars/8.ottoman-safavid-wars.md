The **Ottoman–Persian Wars** also called the **Ottoman–Iranian Wars** were a series of wars between the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) and the [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran), [Afsharid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afsharid_Iran), [Zand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zand_Iran), and [Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran) states of [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran) ([Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persis)) through the 16th–19th centuries. The Ottomans consolidated their control of what is today [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey) in the 15th century, and gradually came into conflict with the emerging neighboring Iranian state, led by [Ismail I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_I) of the [Safavid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty). The two states were arch rivals, and were also divided by religious grounds, the [Ottomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) being staunchly [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) and the [Safavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran) being [Shia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia). A series of military conflicts ensued for centuries during which the two empires competed for control over eastern [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia), the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus), and [Ottoman Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Iraq).

Among the numerous treaties, the [Treaty of Zuhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Zuhab) of 1639 is usually considered as the most significant, as it fixed present [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey)–[Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran) and [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq)–[Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran) borders. In later treaties, there were frequent references to the Treaty of Zuhab.
