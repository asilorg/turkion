**Timur**[b] (1320s – 17/18 February 1405), also known as **Tamerlane** ,[c] was a [Turco-Mongol](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turco-Mongol_tradition> "Turco-Mongol tradition") conqueror, first ruler of the [Timurid dynasty](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty> "Timurid dynasty"), and the founder of the [Timurid Empire](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire> "Timurid Empire"), which ruled over modern-day [Afghanistan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan> "Afghanistan"), [Iran](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran> "Iran"), and [Central Asia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia> "Central Asia"). He was undefeated in battle and is widely regarded as one of the greatest military leaders and tacticians in history, as well as one of the most brutal and deadly.[9][10][11] Timur is also considered a great patron of the arts, for he interacted with scholars and poets such as [ibn Khaldun](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Khaldun> "Ibn Khaldun"), [Hafez](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafez> "Hafez"), and [Hafiz-i Abru](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafiz-i_Abru> "Hafiz-i Abru"). His reign led to the [Timurid Renaissance](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Renaissance> "Timurid Renaissance").[12]

Born into the Turkicized Mongol confederation of the [Barlas](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barlas> "Barlas") in [Transoxiana](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transoxiana> "Transoxiana") (now in [Uzbekistan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan> "Uzbekistan")) in the 1320s, Timur gained control of the western [Chagatai Khanate](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_Khanate> "Chagatai Khanate") by 1370 and from there he led a series of military campaigns defeating the Khans of the [Golden Horde](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde> "Golden Horde"), the [Mamluk Sultanate in Egypt and Syria](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate> "Mamluk Sultanate"), the emerging [Ottoman Empire](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire> "Ottoman Empire"), as well as the [Delhi Sultanate](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate> "Delhi Sultanate") in the [Indian subcontinent](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent> "Indian subcontinent"), thus becoming the most powerful ruler in the [Muslim world](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world> "Muslim world").[13] These conquests led to the creation of the Timurid Empire, which fragmented shortly after his death. He spoke several languages, including the [Karluk Turkic](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karluk_languages> "Karluk languages") language [Chagatai](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_language> "Chagatai language") (an ancestor of modern [Uzbek](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_language> "Uzbek language") and [Uyghur](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_language> "Uyghur language")), as well as [Classical Mongolian](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Mongolian> "Classical Mongolian") and [New Persian](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Persian> "New Persian"), which he used for diplomatic correspondence. 

Timur was the last of the major [nomadic conquerors](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomadic_empire> "Nomadic empire") of the [Eurasian Steppe](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Steppe> "Eurasian Steppe"), and his empire set the stage for the rise of the more organized and lasting Muslim [gunpowder empires](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder_empires> "Gunpowder empires") of the 16th and 17th centuries.[14][15][16] Timur was of both Turkic and Mongol descent, and, while probably not a direct descendant on either side, he shared a common ancestor with [Genghis Khan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan> "Genghis Khan") on his father's side,[17][18][19] though some authors have suggested his mother may have been a descendant of the Khan.[20][21] He clearly sought to revive the legacy of Genghis Khan and saw himself as the restorer of the [Mongol Empire](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire> "Mongol Empire"). According to [Gérard Chaliand](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Chaliand> "Gérard Chaliand"), Timur considered himself Genghis Khan's heir.[22][23]

Timur referred to himself as the "Sword of Islam". He was a patron of religion and the arts, but styled himself a _[ghazi](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_\(warrior\)> "Ghazi \(warrior\)")_ ([Arabic](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language> "Arabic language"): غازي, [romanized](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic> "Romanization of Arabic"):  _ghāzī_ , [lit.](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation> "Literal translation") 'religious warrior') in the last years of his life.[3] By the end of his reign, Timur had gained complete control over all the remnants of the Chagatai Khanate, the [Ilkhanate](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanate> "Ilkhanate"), and the Golden Horde, and had even attempted to restore the [Yuan dynasty](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty> "Yuan dynasty") in China. Timur's armies were multi-ethnic and much feared, and laid waste to sizable parts of Asia, Africa, and Europe.[9][24] Scholars estimate that his military campaigns caused the deaths of millions of people.[25][26] Of all the areas he conquered, [Khwarazm](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwarazm> "Khwarazm") suffered the most, as it repeatedly rose against him.[27] Timur carried out five military campaigns against Khwarazm.[28]

He was the grandfather of the Timurid sultan, astronomer and mathematician [Ulugh Beg](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Beg> "Ulugh Beg"), who ruled Central Asia from 1411 to 1449, and the great-great-great-grandfather of [Babur](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur> "Babur") (1483–1530), founder of the [Mughal Empire](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire> "Mughal Empire").[29][30]

## Ancestry

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Genealogical_Relationship_between_Timur_and_Genghis_Khan.png/250px-Genealogical_Relationship_between_Timur_and_Genghis_Khan.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Genealogical_Relationship_between_Timur_and_Genghis_Khan.png>)The genealogical relationship between Timur and Genghis Khan, according to Timurid sources

Historian [Peter Jackson](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Jackson_\(historian\)> "Peter Jackson \(historian\)") considers it generally accepted that Timur was of "unimpeachable Mongol ancestry". Timurid histories, such as [Yazdi](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharaf_al-Din_Ali_Yazdi> "Sharaf al-Din Ali Yazdi")'s _[Zafarnama](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafarnama_\(Yazdi_biography\)> "Zafarnama \(Yazdi biography\)")_ , [Hafiz-i Abru](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafiz-i_Abru> "Hafiz-i Abru")'s _[Zubdat at-tawarikh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zubdat_al-Tawarikh_\(TIEM_1973\)> "Zubdat al-Tawarikh \(TIEM 1973\)")_ , and the _Muiz al-Ansab_ , trace his paternal descent from [Tumbinai Khan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumbinai_Khan> "Tumbinai Khan"), a male-line ancestor of [Genghis Khan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan> "Genghis Khan").[19][31] However, while [Rashid al-Din](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_al-Din_Hamadani> "Rashid al-Din Hamadani")'s _[Jami' al-tawarikh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jami%27_al-tawarikh> "Jami' al-tawarikh")_ and the _[Secret History of the Mongols](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_History_of_the_Mongols> "Secret History of the Mongols")_ assign a common ancestry with Genghis Khan to [Timur's tribe](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barlas> "Barlas") as a whole, no written evidence from Timur's lifetime or earlier makes such a link to him directly.[32]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Original_location_of_the_Mongol_Barlas_tribe.jpg/250px-Original_location_of_the_Mongol_Barlas_tribe.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Original_location_of_the_Mongol_Barlas_tribe.jpg>)Original location of Timur's Mongol [Barlas](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barlas> "Barlas") tribe

Timur's ancestor [Qarachar Noyan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qarachar_Noyan> "Qarachar Noyan"), written in Timurid works as Tumanay's great-great-grandson, was a military commander under Genghis Khan, and was later assigned to the latter's son [Chagatai](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_Khan> "Chagatai Khan") in [Transoxiana](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transoxiana> "Transoxiana").[33][34] Though there are not many mentions of Qarachar in 13th and 14th century records, later Timurid sources greatly emphasized his role in the early history of the [Mongol Empire](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire> "Mongol Empire").[35][36] These histories also state that Genghis Khan later established the "bond of fatherhood and sonship" by marrying Chagatai's daughter to Qarachar.[37] Through his claimed descent from this marriage, Timur could assert kinship with the [Chagatai Khans](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_Khanate> "Chagatai Khanate").[38]

The origins of Timur's mother, Tekina Khatun, are less clear. The _Zafarnama_ merely states her name without giving any information regarding her background. Writing in 1403, [John III](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_III_of_Soltaniyeh> "John III of Soltaniyeh"), [Archbishop](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop> "Archbishop") of [Sultaniyya](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soltaniyeh> "Soltaniyeh"), claimed that she was of lowly origin.[33] The _Mu'izz al-Ansab_ , written decades later, says that she was related to the [Yasa'uri](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasa%27ur> "Yasa'ur") tribe, whose lands bordered that of the Barlas.[39] [Ibn Khaldun](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Khaldun> "Ibn Khaldun") recounted that Timur himself described to him his mother's descent from the legendary [Persian](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people> "Persian people") hero [Manuchehr](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuchehr> "Manuchehr").[40] [Ibn Arabshah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Arabshah> "Ahmad ibn Arabshah") suggested that she was a descendant of Genghis Khan.[21] The 18th century _Books of Timur_ identify her as the daughter of 'Sadr al-Sharia', which is believed to refer to the [Hanafi](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafi> "Hanafi") scholar Ubayd Allah al-Mahbubi of [Bukhara](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukhara> "Bukhara").[41]

Practically all of Timur’s contemporaries identified him as a Mongol, including [Ibn Khaldun](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Khaldun> "Ibn Khaldun"), who personally met Timur in Damascus in 1401, the Arab historian [Ibn Arabshah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Arabshah> "Ibn Arabshah"), and the Castilian envoy [Ruy González de Clavijo](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruy_Gonz%C3%A1lez_de_Clavijo> "Ruy González de Clavijo"), who met Timur in 1404. In his work describing his encounter with Timur, Ibn Khaldun refers to him as the "sultan of the Mongols (_Mughul_) and Tatars (_Ṭaṭar_)", and describes the language of his court as the "Mongol language (_al-lisān al-Mughulī_)". He also uses the term "Chaghatay" (_Jaqatay_) alongside the designation "Mongol" (_Mughul_) when referring to Timur and his commanders.[42] Clavijo also reports that after the death of [Chagatai Khan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_Khan> "Chagatai Khan") (r. 1227–1242), the Timurid nomads began calling themselves Chaghatays and that they traced their origins to the [Mongols of Genghis Khan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire> "Mongol Empire") (_Tartaros_). Ibn Arabshah refers to Timur’s army as "Tatars" and, quoting Timur’s own words, portrays both him and the [Qara Tatars](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qara_Tatars> "Qara Tatars")—a surviving group of [Ilkhanid Mongols](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanate> "Ilkhanate") who lived in Anatolia—as belonging to the same people.[42] The term _Mughul_ was never used in the contemporary sense to refer to a [speaker of the Mongolian language](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols> "Mongols"). In the _[Zafarnama](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafarnama_\(Yazdi_biography\)> "Zafarnama \(Yazdi biography\)")_ , the Mongols who lived in the territory of modern-day [Mongolia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia> "Mongolia") at the turn of the 15th century are referred to as _Qalmaq_ , not _Mughul_.[43]

Nevertheless, Timur and his descendants (the [Timurids](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty> "Timurid dynasty")) regarded themselves as belonging to the [Mongol ulus](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire> "Mongol Empire") and were perceived as such by their contemporaries. They identified themselves with the Chagatay Mongols, and those around them likewise recognized them as such. Timur’s Mongol identity was not constructed as an attempt to legitimize his rule; it reflected his ethnic origin and product of his Mongol descent.[44]

## Early life

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Timur_seated_%28earliest_known_portrait%29%2C_Timurid_genealogy%2C_1405-1409%2C_Samarkand_%28TSMK%2C_H2152%29.jpg/250px-Timur_seated_%28earliest_known_portrait%29%2C_Timurid_genealogy%2C_1405-1409%2C_Samarkand_%28TSMK%2C_H2152%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Timur_seated_\(earliest_known_portrait\),_Timurid_genealogy,_1405-1409,_Samarkand_\(TSMK,_H2152\).jpg>)Earliest known portrait of Timur, commissioned right after his death in 1405–1409, by his grandson [Khalil Sultan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalil_Sultan> "Khalil Sultan") for an illustrated Timurid genealogy (TSMK, H2152).[45]

Timur was born in Transoxiana near the city of Kesh (now [Shahrisabz](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahrisabz> "Shahrisabz"), Uzbekistan), some 80 kilometres (50 mi) south of [Samarkand](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarkand> "Samarkand"), part of what was then the Chagatai Khanate.[_[citation needed](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed> "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] His name, _Temur_ , means "[iron](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron> "Iron")" in Chagatai, his mother tongue (cf. Uzbek _Temir_ , [Turkish](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language> "Turkish language") _Demir_).[46] It is likely cognate with [Genghis Khan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan> "Genghis Khan")'s birth name, _Temüjin_ , a loan from Common Turkic *_temürči(n)_ "blacksmith".[47][48]

Later Timurid dynastic histories claim that Timur was born on 8 April 1336, but most sources from his lifetime give ages that are consistent with a birthdate in the late 1320s. Several scholars suspect that the 1336 date was intended to link Timur to the legacy of [Abu Sa'id Bahadur Khan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Sa%27id_Bahadur_Khan> "Abu Sa'id Bahadur Khan"), the last ruler of the [Ilkhanate](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanate> "Ilkhanate") and a descendant of [Hulegu Khan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulegu_Khan> "Hulegu Khan"), who died in that year.[49][50]

Timur was a member of the Barlas, a [Mongol tribe](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols> "Mongols")[51][52] that had been [turkified](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkification> "Turkification") in many aspects.[53][54][55][56][57] His father, [Taraghai](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taraghai> "Taraghai"), was described as a minor noble of this tribe. However, Manz argues that Timur may have later understated his father's social position to make his own successes appear more remarkable. She states that though he is not believed to have been especially powerful, Taraghai was reasonably wealthy and influential.[50]: 116 This is shown in the [_Zafarnama_](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafarnama_\(Yazdi_biography\)> "Zafarnama \(Yazdi biography\)"), which states that Timur later returning to his birthplace following the death of his father on 12 March 1360 AD, suggesting concern over his estate.[58] Taraghai's social significance is further hinted at by [Arabshah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Arabshah> "Ahmad ibn Arabshah"), who described him as a magnate in the court of [Amir Husayn](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Husayn> "Amir Husayn") [Qara'unas](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qara%27unas> "Qara'unas").[21] In addition to this, the father of the great Amir Hamid Kereyid of [Moghulistan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moghulistan> "Moghulistan") is stated as a friend of Taraghai's.[59]

In his childhood, Timur and a small band of followers [raided](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raid_\(military\)> "Raid \(military\)") travellers for goods, especially animals such as sheep, horses, and cattle.[50]: 116 Around 1363, it is believed that Timur tried to steal a sheep from a shepherd but was shot by two arrows, one in his right leg and another in his right hand, where he lost two fingers. Both injuries disabled him for life. Some believe that these injuries occurred while serving as a mercenary to the khan of [Sistan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistan> "Sistan") in what is today the [Dasht-e Margo](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasht-e_Margo> "Dasht-e Margo") in southwest [Afghanistan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan> "Afghanistan"). Timur's injuries and disability gave rise to the nickname "Timur the Lame" or _Temūr(-i) Lang_ in Persian, which is the origin of Tamerlane, the name by which he is generally known in the West.[60]

By about 1360, Timur had gained prominence as a military leader whose troops were mostly Turkic tribesmen of the region.[23] He took part in campaigns in Transoxiana with the Khan of the Chagatai Khanate. Allying himself both in cause and by family connection with [Amir Qazaghan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Qazaghan> "Amir Qazaghan"), the dethroner and destroyer of [Volga Bulgaria](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Bulgaria> "Volga Bulgaria"), he invaded [Khorasan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorasan> "Khorasan")[61] at the head of a thousand horsemen. This was the second military expedition that he led, and its success led to further operations, among them the subjugation of [Khwarazm](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwarazm> "Khwarazm") and [Urgench](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urgench> "Urgench") in the [Siege of Urgench (1379)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Urgench_\(1379\)> "Siege of Urgench \(1379\)").[62]

Following Qazaghan's murder, disputes arose among the many claimants to [sovereign](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign> "Sovereign") power. [Tughlugh Timur](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tughlugh_Timur> "Tughlugh Timur") of [Kashgar](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashgar> "Kashgar"), the Khan of the Eastern Chagatai Khanate, another descendant of Genghis Khan, invaded, interrupting this infighting. Timur was sent to negotiate with the invader but instead joined him and was rewarded with Transoxiana. Around this time, his father died, and Timur also became the chief of the Barlas. Tughlugh then attempted to set his son [Ilyas Khoja](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyas_Khoja> "Ilyas Khoja") over Transoxiana, but Timur repelled this invasion with a smaller force.[61]

## Rise to power

In this period, Timur reduced the [Chagatai khans](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_Khanate> "Chagatai Khanate") to the position of [figureheads](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figurehead> "Figurehead") while he ruled in their name. Also during this period, Timur and his brother-in-law [Amir Husayn](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Husayn> "Amir Husayn"), who were at first fellow fugitives and wanderers, became rivals and antagonists.[62] The relationship between them became strained after Husayn abandoned efforts to carry out Timur's orders to finish off Ilya Khoja (former governor of Mawarannah) close to [Tashkent](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashkent> "Tashkent").[63]

Timur gained followers in Balkh, consisting of merchants, fellow tribesmen, Muslim clergy, aristocracy and agricultural workers, because of his kindness in sharing his belongings with them. This contrasted Timur's behavior with that of Husayn, who alienated these people, took many possessions from them via his heavy tax laws and selfishly spent the tax money building elaborate structures.[64] Around 1370, Husayn surrendered to Timur and was later assassinated, which allowed Timur to be formally proclaimed sovereign at [Balkh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkh> "Balkh"). He married Husayn's wife [Saray Mulk Khanum](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saray_Mulk_Khanum> "Saray Mulk Khanum"), a descendant of Genghis Khan, allowing him to become imperial ruler of the Chaghatay tribe.[9]

## Legitimization of Timur's rule

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Depiction_of_Timur_granting_audience_on_the_occasion_of_his_accession%2C_in_the_near_contemporary_Zafarnama_%281424-1428%29%2C_1467_edition.jpg/500px-Depiction_of_Timur_granting_audience_on_the_occasion_of_his_accession%2C_in_the_near_contemporary_Zafarnama_%281424-1428%29%2C_1467_edition.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Depiction_of_Timur_granting_audience_on_the_occasion_of_his_accession,_in_the_near_contemporary_Zafarnama_\(1424-1428\),_1467_edition.jpg>)Depiction of Timur granting audience on the occasion of his accession in [Balkh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkh> "Balkh") in 1370. _[Zafarnama](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafarnama_\(Yazdi_biography\)> "Zafarnama \(Yazdi biography\)")_ (1467), commissioned by [Sultan Husayn Bayqara](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Husayn_Bayqara> "Sultan Husayn Bayqara")

Timur's Turco-Mongolian heritage provided opportunities and challenges as he sought to rule the Mongol Empire and the Muslim world.[50] According to the Mongol traditions, Timur could not claim the title of _khan_ or rule the Mongol Empire because he was not a descendant of [Genghis Khan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan> "Genghis Khan"). Therefore, Timur set up a puppet Chaghatayid Khan, [Suyurghatmish](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyurgatmish> "Soyurgatmish"), as the nominal ruler of Balkh as he pretended to act as a "protector of the member of a Chinggisid line, that of Genghis Khan's eldest son, [Jochi](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jochi> "Jochi")".[65] Timur instead used the title of [Amir](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir> "Amir") meaning general, and acting in the name of the [Chagatai](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_Khanate> "Chagatai Khanate") ruler of Transoxiana.[50]: 106 To reinforce this position, Timur claimed the title _güregen_ (royal son-in-law) to a princess of Chinggisid line.[2]

As with the title of Khan, Timur similarly could not claim the supreme title of the Islamic world, [Caliph](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph> "Caliph"), because the "office was limited to the [Quraysh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quraysh> "Quraysh"), the tribe of the [Prophet Muhammad](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophet_Muhammad> "Prophet Muhammad")". Therefore, Timur reacted to the challenge by creating a myth and image of himself as a "supernatural personal power" ordained by God.[65] Timur's most famous title was _Sahib Qiran_ (صَاحِبِ قِرَان, 'Lord of Conjunction'), which is rooted in [astrology](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrology> "Astrology")[66] a title that was used before him to designate [Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamza_ibn_Abd_al-Muttalib> "Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib"), the paternal uncle of Muhammad[3] and which was taken by the Mamluk Sultan [Baybars](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baybars> "Baybars") and by various rulers of the [Ilkhanate](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanate> "Ilkhanate") to designate themselves.[3] In that regard, he simply pursued an existing tradition in the [Muslim world](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world> "Muslim world") to designate conquerors.[3]

The title was referring to the conjunction of the two "superior planets", Saturn and Jupiter, which was held to be an auspicious sign and the mark of a new era.[66] According to A. Azfar Moin, _Sahib Qiran_ was a messianic title, implying that Timur might potentially be the "awaited messiah descended from the prophetic line" who would "inaugurate a new era, possibly the last one before the end of time."[66] Otherwise he depicted himself as a spiritual descendant of Ali, thus claiming the lineage of both Genghis Khan and the Quraysh.[67]

## Period of expansion

Timur spent the next 35 years in various wars and expeditions. He not only consolidated his rule at home by the subjugation of his foes, but sought extension of territory by encroachments upon the lands of foreign potentates. His conquests to the west and northwest led him to the lands near the [Caspian Sea](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_Sea> "Caspian Sea") and to the banks of the [Ural](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_River> "Ural River") and the [Volga](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga> "Volga"). Conquests in the south and southwest encompassed almost every province in [Persia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia> "Persia"), including [Baghdad](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad> "Baghdad"), [Karbala](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karbala> "Karbala") and Northern Iraq.[62]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Timur%27s_army%2C_commanded_by_Umar_Shaykh%2C_attacks_Urgench_in_1379._Garrett_Zafarnama%2C_painted_in_1480.jpg/330px-Timur%27s_army%2C_commanded_by_Umar_Shaykh%2C_attacks_Urgench_in_1379._Garrett_Zafarnama%2C_painted_in_1480.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Timur%27s_army,_commanded_by_Umar_Shaykh,_attacks_Urgench_in_1379._Garrett_Zafarnama,_painted_in_1480.jpg>)Timur's army, commanded by his son [Umar Shaykh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umar_Shaikh_Mirza_I> "Umar Shaikh Mirza I"), in the [Siege of Urgench (1379)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Urgench_\(1379\)> "Siege of Urgench \(1379\)"). _Garrett Zafarnama_ (1480)

One of the most formidable of Timur's opponents was another Mongol ruler, a descendant of Genghis Khan named [Tokhtamysh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokhtamysh> "Tokhtamysh"). After having been a refugee in Timur's court, Tokhtamysh became ruler both of the eastern [Kipchak](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Horde> "White Horde") and the [Golden Horde](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde> "Golden Horde"). After his accession, he quarreled with Timur over the possession of [Khwarizm](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khiva> "Khiva") and [Azerbaijan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan> "Azerbaijan").[62] However, Timur still supported him against the Russians, and in 1382, Tokhtamysh invaded the Muscovite dominion and [burned Moscow](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Moscow_\(1382\)> "Siege of Moscow \(1382\)").[68]

[Russian Orthodox](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church> "Russian Orthodox Church") tradition states that later, in 1395, having reached the frontier of the [Principality of Ryazan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Ryazan> "Principality of Ryazan"), Timur had taken [Yelets](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yelets> "Yelets") and started advancing towards Moscow. [Vasily I of Moscow](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_I_of_Moscow> "Vasily I of Moscow") went with an army to [Kolomna](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolomna> "Kolomna") and halted at the banks of the [Oka River](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oka_\(river\)> "Oka \(river\)"). The clergy brought the famed [Theotokos of Vladimir](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theotokos_of_Vladimir> "Theotokos of Vladimir") icon from [Vladimir](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir,_Russia> "Vladimir, Russia") to Moscow.[69] Timur stopped his advance and withdrew from Russian territory, with the Russian chroniclers claiming that a vision of the [Virgin](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Mary> "Virgin Mary") defending Moscow, accompanied by a heavenly host, convinced him to turn back.[69]

## Conquest of Persia

After the death of [Abu Sa'id](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Sa%27id_\(Ilkhanid_dynasty\)> "Abu Sa'id \(Ilkhanid dynasty\)"), ruler of the [Ilkhanate](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanate> "Ilkhanate"), in 1335, there was a power vacuum in Persia. In the end, Persia was split amongst the [Muzaffarids](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarids_\(Iran\)> "Muzaffarids \(Iran\)"), [Kartids](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartids> "Kartids"), [Eretnids](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eretnids> "Eretnids"), [Chobanids](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chobanids> "Chobanids"), [Injuids](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injuids> "Injuids"), [Jalayirids](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalayirids> "Jalayirids"), and [Sarbadars](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarbadars> "Sarbadars"). In 1383, Timur started his lengthy military conquest of Persia, though he already ruled over much of Persian [Khorasan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khorasan> "Greater Khorasan") by 1381, after Khwaja Mas'ud, of the [Sarbadar](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarbadar> "Sarbadar") dynasty surrendered. Timur began his Persian campaign with [Herat](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herat> "Herat"), capital of the [Kartid dynasty](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_dynasty> "Kurt dynasty"). When Herat did not surrender he reduced the city to rubble and massacred most of its citizens; it remained in ruins until [Shah Rukh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Rukh> "Shah Rukh") ordered its reconstruction around 1415.[70] Timur then sent a general to capture rebellious [Kandahar](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandahar> "Kandahar"). With the capture of Herat the Kartid kingdom surrendered and became vassals of Timur; it would later be annexed outright less than a decade later in 1389 by Timur's son [Miran Shah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miran_Shah> "Miran Shah").[71]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/Empire_of_Timur_and_his_Campaigns.png/330px-Empire_of_Timur_and_his_Campaigns.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Empire_of_Timur_and_his_Campaigns.png>)Timur's empire and his military campaigns

Timur then headed west to capture the [Zagros Mountains](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagros_Mountains> "Zagros Mountains"), passing through [Mazandaran](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran> "Mazandaran"). During his travel through the north of Persia, he captured the then town of [Tehran](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran> "Tehran"), which surrendered and was thus treated mercifully. He laid siege to [Soltaniyeh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soltaniyeh> "Soltaniyeh") in 1384. [Khorasan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khorasan> "Greater Khorasan") revolted one year later, so Timur destroyed Isfizar, and the prisoners were cemented into the walls alive. The next year the kingdom of Sistan, under the [Mihrabanid dynasty](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihrabanids> "Mihrabanids"), was ravaged, and its capital at [Zaranj](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaranj> "Zaranj") was destroyed. Timur then returned to his capital of [Samarkand](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarkand> "Samarkand"), where he began planning for his [Georgian campaign](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur%27s_invasion_of_Georgia> "Timur's invasion of Georgia") and [Golden Horde](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokhtamysh%E2%80%93Timur_war> "Tokhtamysh–Timur war") invasion. In 1386, Timur passed through Mazandaran as he had when trying to capture the Zagros. He went near the city of Soltaniyeh, which he had previously captured but instead turned north and captured [Tabriz](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabriz> "Tabriz") with little resistance, along with [Maragha](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maragha> "Maragha").[72] He ordered heavy taxation of the people, which was collected by Adil Aqa, who was also given control over Soltaniyeh. Adil was later executed because Timur suspected him of corruption.[73]

Timur then went north to begin his Georgian and Golden Horde campaigns, pausing his full-scale invasion of Persia. When he returned, he found his generals had done well in protecting the cities and lands he had conquered in Persia.[74] Though many rebelled, and his son [Miran Shah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miran_Shah> "Miran Shah"), who may have been [regent](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent> "Regent"), was forced to annex rebellious vassal dynasties, his holdings remained. So he proceeded to capture the rest of Persia, specifically the two major southern cities of [Isfahan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isfahan> "Isfahan") and [Shiraz](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz> "Shiraz"). When he arrived with his army at Isfahan in 1387, the city [immediately surrendered](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Isfahan_\(1387\)> "Siege of Isfahan \(1387\)"); he treated it with relative mercy as he normally did with cities that surrendered (unlike Herat).[75] However, after Isfahan revolted against Timur's taxes by killing the tax collectors and some of Timur's soldiers, he ordered the massacre of the city's citizens; the death toll is reckoned at between 100,000 and 200,000.[76] An eye-witness counted more than 28 towers constructed of about 1,500 heads each.[77] This has been described as a "systematic use of terror against towns...an integral element of Tamerlane's strategic element", which he viewed as preventing bloodshed by discouraging resistance. His massacres were selective and he spared the artistic and educated.[76] This would later influence the next great Persian conqueror: [Nader Shah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah> "Nader Shah").[78]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Timur_coin._Citing_Chagatai_khan_Mahmud._Baghdad_mint._Dated_AH_795_%28AD_1393%29.jpg/250px-Timur_coin._Citing_Chagatai_khan_Mahmud._Baghdad_mint._Dated_AH_795_%28AD_1393%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Timur_coin._Citing_Chagatai_khan_Mahmud._Baghdad_mint._Dated_AH_795_\(AD_1393\).jpg>)Rare coinage of Timur while in [Jalayirid](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalayirid> "Jalayirid") Baghdad, following the [Siege of Baghdad (1393)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Baghdad_\(1393\)> "Siege of Baghdad \(1393\)"). Citing [Chagatai](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_Khanate> "Chagatai Khanate") khan [Mahmud](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Mahmud_\(Chagatai\)> "Sultan Mahmud \(Chagatai\)").

Timur then began a five-year campaign to the west in 1392, attacking [Persian Kurdistan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Kurdistan> "Iranian Kurdistan").[79][80][81] In 1393, Shiraz was captured after surrendering, and the Muzaffarids became vassals of Timur, though prince [Shah Mansur](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Mansur_\(Persia\)> "Shah Mansur \(Persia\)") rebelled but was defeated at the [Battle of Shiraz (1393)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shiraz_\(1393\)> "Battle of Shiraz \(1393\)"), and the [Muzafarids](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzafarids> "Muzafarids") were annexed. Shortly after Georgia was devastated so that the Golden Horde could not use it to threaten northern Iran.[82] In the same year, Timur [caught Baghdad](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Baghdad_\(1393\)> "Siege of Baghdad \(1393\)") by surprise in August by marching there in only eight days from Shiraz. Sultan [Ahmad Jalayir](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Jalayir> "Ahmad Jalayir") fled to Syria, where the Mamluk Sultan [Barquq](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barquq> "Barquq") protected him and killed Timur's envoys. Timur left the [Sarbadar](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarbadar> "Sarbadar") prince Khwaja Mas'ud to govern [Baghdad](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad> "Baghdad"), but he was [driven out](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Baghdad_\(1394\)> "Capture of Baghdad \(1394\)") when Ahmad Jalayir returned. Ahmad was unpopular but got help from [Qara Yusuf](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qara_Yusuf> "Qara Yusuf") of the [Kara Koyunlu](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_Koyunlu> "Kara Koyunlu"); he fled again in 1399, this time to the Ottomans.[83]

## Tokhtamysh–Timur war

See also: [Karsakpay inscription](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karsakpay_inscription> "Karsakpay inscription")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Battle_between_Timur_and_Toqtamish_Khan%2C_1420-1440%2C_possibly_Herat._Topkapi_H.2153.jpg/330px-Battle_between_Timur_and_Toqtamish_Khan%2C_1420-1440%2C_possibly_Herat._Topkapi_H.2153.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Battle_between_Timur_and_Toqtamish_Khan,_1420-1440,_possibly_Herat._Topkapi_H.2153.jpg>)Battle between Timur (left) and [Tokhtamysh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokhtamysh> "Tokhtamysh") (right), 1420–1440, possibly [Herat](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herat> "Herat"). Topkapi H.2153.

In the meantime, Tokhtamysh, now khan of the [Golden Horde](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde> "Golden Horde"), turned against his patron and in 1385 invaded [Azerbaijan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan> "Azerbaijan"). The inevitable response by Timur resulted in the [Tokhtamysh–Timur war](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokhtamysh%E2%80%93Timur_war> "Tokhtamysh–Timur war"). In the initial stage of the war, Timur won a victory at the [Battle of the Kondurcha River](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Kondurcha_River> "Battle of the Kondurcha River"). After the battle Tokhtamysh and some of his army were allowed to escape. After Tokhtamysh's initial defeat, Timur invaded Muscovy to the north of Tokhtamysh's holdings. Timur's army burned [Ryazan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazan> "Ryazan") and advanced on Moscow. He was pulled away before reaching the Oka River by Tokhtamysh's renewed campaign in the south.[84]

In the first phase of the conflict with Tokhtamysh, Timur led an army of over 100,000 men north for more than 700 miles into the steppe. He then rode west about 1,000 miles advancing in a front more than 10 miles wide. During this advance, Timur's army got far enough north to be in a region of [very long summer days](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_sun> "Midnight sun") causing complaints by his Muslim soldiers about keeping a long schedule of [prayers](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salat> "Salat"). It was then that Tokhtamysh's army was boxed in against the east bank of the Volga River in the [Orenburg](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orenburg> "Orenburg") region and destroyed at the [Battle of the Kondurcha River](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Kondurcha_River> "Battle of the Kondurcha River"), in 1391. 

In the second phase of the conflict, Timur took a different route against the enemy by invading the realm of Tokhtamysh via the [Caucasus](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus> "Caucasus") region. In 1395, Timur defeated Tokhtamysh in the [Battle of the Terek River](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Terek_River> "Battle of the Terek River"), concluding the struggle between the two monarchs. Tokhtamysh was unable to restore his power or prestige, and he was killed about a decade later in the area of present-day [Tyumen](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyumen> "Tyumen"). During the course of Timur's campaigns, his army destroyed [Sarai](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarai_\(city\)> "Sarai \(city\)"), the capital of the Golden Horde, and [Astrakhan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrakhan> "Astrakhan"), subsequently disrupting the Golden Horde's [Silk Road](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road> "Silk Road"). The Golden Horde never recovered from its losses to Timur and ultimately disintegrated into a number of smaller khanates within the next half-century, which allowed [Moscow](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Principality_of_Moscow> "Grand Principality of Moscow") to continue the "gathering of the Russian lands" and emerge as the dominant [Russian state](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_principalities> "Russian principalities").[85]

### Ismailis

In May 1393, Timur's army invaded the [Anjudan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anjudan> "Anjudan"), crippling the [Ismaili](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismaili> "Ismaili") village only a year after his assault on the Ismailis in [Mazandaran](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran> "Mazandaran"). The village was prepared for the attack, evidenced by its fortress and system of tunnels. Undeterred, Timur's soldiers flooded the tunnels by cutting into a channel overhead. Timur's reasons for attacking this village are not yet well understood. However, it has been suggested that his religious persuasions and view of himself as an executor of divine will may have contributed to his motivations.[86] The Persian historian [Khwandamir](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwandamir> "Khwandamir") explains that an Ismaili presence was growing more politically powerful in Persian [Iraq](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq> "Iraq"). A group of locals in the region was dissatisfied with this and, Khwandamir writes, these locals assembled and brought up their complaint with Timur, possibly provoking his attack on the Ismailis there.[86]

In the late 14th century, the [Tughlaq dynasty](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tughlaq_dynasty> "Tughlaq dynasty"), which had ruled the [Delhi Sultanate](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate> "Delhi Sultanate") since 1320, had declined. Most provincial governors had asserted their independence, and the Sultanate was reduced to a fraction of its former extent.[87] This anarchy drew the attention of Timur, who in 1398 invaded the Indian subcontinent during the reign of Sultan [Mahmud Shah II](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_Shah_II> "Mahmud Shah II"). After crossing the [Indus River](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River> "Indus River") on 30 September 1398 with a force of 90,000, he sacked [Tulamba](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulamba> "Tulamba") and massacred its inhabitants.[87] He sent an advance guard under his grandson [Pir Muhammad](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pir_Muhammad_\(son_of_Umar_Shaikh\)> "Pir Muhammad \(son of Umar Shaikh\)"), who [captured Multan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Multan_\(1398\)> "Siege of Multan \(1398\)") after a siege of six months.[87] His invasion was unopposed as most of the nobility surrendered without a fight. He encountered resistance from a force of 2000 under Malik [Jasrat](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasrat> "Jasrat") at the [Sutlej](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutlej> "Sutlej") between Tulamba and [Dipalpur](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipalpur> "Dipalpur"). Jasrat was defeated and taken into captivity.[88][87] Next he [captured the fort of Bhatner](<https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Siege_of_Bhatner&action=edit&redlink=1> "Siege of Bhatner \(page does not exist\)") which was being defended by [Rajput](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajput> "Rajput") chief Rai Dul Chand and demolished it.[89]

While on his march towards Delhi, Timur was opposed by the [Jat](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jat> "Jat") peasantry, who would loot caravans and then disappear in the forests. He had thousands of Jats killed and many taken captive.[90][91] But the Sultanate at Delhi did nothing to stop his advance.[92]

### Capture of Delhi (1398)

Main article: [Sack of Delhi (1398)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Delhi_\(1398\)> "Sack of Delhi \(1398\)")

The battle took place on 17 December 1398. Before the battle, Timur slaughtered some 100,000 slaves who had been captured previously in the Indian campaign. This was done out of fear that they might revolt.[93]

Sultan Nasir-ud-Din Mahmud Shah Tughluq and the army of Mallu Iqbal had war elephants armored with chain mail and poison on their tusks.[94] As his Tatar forces were afraid of the elephants, Timur ordered his men to dig a trench in front of their positions. Timur then loaded his camels with as much wood and hay as they could carry. When the war elephants charged, Timur set the hay on fire and prodded the camels with iron sticks, causing them to charge at the elephants, howling in pain: Timur had understood that elephants were easily panicked. Faced with the strange spectacle of camels flying straight at them with flames leaping from their backs, the elephants turned around and stampeded back toward their own lines. Timur capitalized on the subsequent disruption in the forces of Nasir-ud-Din Mahmud Shah Tughluq, securing an easy victory. Nasir-ud-Din Mahmud Shah Tughluq fled with remnants of his forces.[95][96][97]

The capture of the [Delhi Sultanate](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate> "Delhi Sultanate") was one of Timur's largest and most devastating victories as at that time, Delhi was one of the richest cities in the world. The city of Delhi was sacked and reduced to ruins, with the population enslaved.[98] After the fall of the city, uprisings by its citizens against the Turkic-Mongols began to occur, causing a retaliatory bloody massacre within the city walls. After three days of citizens uprising within Delhi, it was said that the city reeked of the decomposing bodies of its citizens with their heads being erected like structures and the bodies left as food for the birds by Timur's soldiers. Timur's invasion and destruction of Delhi continued the chaos that was still consuming India, and the city would not be able to recover from the great loss it suffered for almost a century.[99]

## Campaigns in the Caucasus and the Levant

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Timur%27s_army_attacks_Nerges%2C_Georgia.jpg/250px-Timur%27s_army_attacks_Nerges%2C_Georgia.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Timur%27s_army_attacks_Nerges,_Georgia.jpg>)Emir Timur's army attacks the survivors of the town of [Nerges](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mingrelia> "Mingrelia"), in Georgia, in the spring of 1396. [_Garrett Zafarnama_](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Garrett_Zafarnama> "commons:Category:Garrett Zafarnama") (c. 1480)

Before the end of 1399, Timur started a war with [Bayezid I](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayezid_I> "Bayezid I"), sultan of the Ottoman Empire, and the [Mamluk](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk> "Mamluk") sultan of Egypt [Nasir-ad-Din Faraj](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasir-ad-Din_Faraj> "Nasir-ad-Din Faraj"). Bayezid began annexing the territory of Turkmen and Muslim rulers in [Anatolia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia> "Anatolia"). As Timur claimed sovereignty over the [Turkoman rulers](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolian_Beyliks> "Anatolian Beyliks"), they took refuge behind him. 

In 1400, Timur [invaded](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur%27s_invasions_of_Georgia> "Timur's invasions of Georgia") [Armenia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia> "Armenia") and [Georgia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_\(country\)> "Georgia \(country\)"). Between 1386 and 1404, the Turco-Mongol forces of Timur raided the countries of [Transcaucasia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasia> "Transcaucasia") from their bases in northern Iran several times. Tiflis was finally conquered by Timur in 1404, and [King George VII](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_VII_of_Georgia> "George VII of Georgia") was forced to recognize Timurid suzerainty. Armenia too, which had been under Mongol [Jalayirid](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalayirid_Sultanate> "Jalayirid Sultanate") control, was incorporated into the Timurid realm.[100] Of the surviving population, more than 60,000 [of the local people](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoples_of_the_Caucasus> "Peoples of the Caucasus") were captured as slaves, and many districts were depopulated.[101] He also sacked [Sivas](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sivas> "Sivas") in Asia Minor.[102]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Jalayirids%2C_Timurids_and_Qara_Qoyunlu_in_1400.png/250px-Jalayirids%2C_Timurids_and_Qara_Qoyunlu_in_1400.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Jalayirids,_Timurids_and_Qara_Qoyunlu_in_1400.png>)[Jalayirid](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalayirid> "Jalayirid") and [Timurid](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty> "Timurid dynasty") territories in 1400.[103]

Then Timur turned his attention to Syria, sacking [Aleppo](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Aleppo_\(1400\)> "Sack of Aleppo \(1400\)"),[104] and [Damascus](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Damascus_\(1400\)> "Siege of Damascus \(1400\)").[105] The city's inhabitants were massacred, except for the artisans, who were deported to Samarkand. 

Timur invaded [Baghdad](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad> "Baghdad") in June 1401. After the capture of the city, 20,000 of its citizens were massacred. Timur ordered that every soldier should return with at least two severed human heads to show him. When they ran out of men to kill, many warriors killed prisoners captured earlier in the campaign, and when they ran out of prisoners to kill, many resorted to beheading their own wives.[106] British historian [David Nicolle](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Nicolle> "David Nicolle"), in his "The Mongol Warlords", quotes an anonymous contemporary historian who compared Timur's army to "ants and locusts covering the whole countryside, plundering and ravaging."[107]

## Invasion of Anatolia

Main articles: [Battle of Ankara](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ankara> "Battle of Ankara") and [Ottoman Interregnum](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Interregnum> "Ottoman Interregnum")

In the meantime, years of insulting letters had passed between Timur and Bayezid. Both rulers insulted each other in their own way while Timur preferred to undermine Bayezid's position as a ruler and play down the significance of his military successes. 

This is the excerpt from one of Timur's letters addressed to the Ottoman sultan: 

> Believe me, you are but pismire ant: don't seek to fight the elephants for they'll crush you under their feet. Shall a petty prince such as you contend with us? But your [rodomontades](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodomontade> "Rodomontade") (braggadocio) are not extraordinary; for a [Turcoman](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkoman_\(ethnonym\)> "Turkoman \(ethnonym\)") never spake with judgement. If you don't follow our counsels you will regret it[108]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Festivities_with_Bayezid_as_prisoner%2C_after_conquering_Rum_territories._Zafarnama%2C_1528_%28Golestan%2C_MS_708%29.jpg/250px-Festivities_with_Bayezid_as_prisoner%2C_after_conquering_Rum_territories._Zafarnama%2C_1528_%28Golestan%2C_MS_708%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Festivities_with_Bayezid_as_prisoner,_after_conquering_Rum_territories._Zafarnama,_1528_\(Golestan,_MS_708\).jpg>)Festivities with [Bayezid I](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayezid_I> "Bayezid I") as prisoner, after conquering Anatolia. [_Zafarnama_ , 1528 (Golestan, MS 708)](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Zafarnama,_1528_\(Golestan,_MS_708\)> "commons:Category:Zafarnama, 1528 \(Golestan, MS 708\)").[109]

Finally, Timur invaded Anatolia and defeated Bayezid in the [Battle of Ankara](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ankara> "Battle of Ankara") on 20 July 1402. Bayezid was captured in battle and subsequently died in captivity, initiating the twelve-year [Ottoman Interregnum](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Interregnum> "Ottoman Interregnum") period. Timur's stated motivation for attacking Bayezid and the Ottoman Empire was the restoration of [Seljuq](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuq_dynasty> "Seljuq dynasty") authority. Timur saw the Seljuks as the rightful rulers of [Anatolia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia> "Anatolia") as they had been granted rule by Mongol conquerors, illustrating Timur's interest with Genghizid legitimacy.[_[citation needed](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed> "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

In December 1402, Timur [besieged and conquered Smyrna](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Smyrna> "Siege of Smyrna"), a stronghold of the Christian [Knights Hospitalers](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Hospitalers> "Knights Hospitalers"); thus he referred to himself as _[ghazi](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_\(warrior\)> "Ghazi \(warrior\)")_ or "Warrior of Islam". A mass beheading was carried out in Smyrna by Timur's soldiers.[110][111][112][113]

With the [Treaty of Gallipoli](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gallipoli> "Treaty of Gallipoli") in February 1403, Timur was furious with the [Genoese](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa> "Republic of Genoa") and [Venetians](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice> "Republic of Venice"), since their ships ferried the Ottoman army to safety in [Thrace](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrace> "Thrace"). As [Lord Kinross](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kinross> "Lord Kinross") reported in _The Ottoman Centuries_ , the Italians preferred the enemy they could handle to the one they could not.[_[citation needed](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed> "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

During the early interregnum, Bayezid I's son _[Mehmed Çelebi](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_I> "Mehmed I")_ acted as Timur's vassal. Unlike other princes, Mehmed minted coins that had Timur's name stamped as "Demur han Gürgân" (تيمور خان كركان), alongside his own as "Mehmed bin Bayezid han" (محمد بن بايزيد خان).[114][115] This was probably an attempt on Mehmed's part to justify to Timur his conquest of [Bursa](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursa> "Bursa") after the [Battle of Ulubad](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ulubad> "Battle of Ulubad"). After Mehmed established himself in _Rum_ , Timur had already begun preparations for his return to Central Asia, and took no further steps to interfere with the _status quo_ in Anatolia.[114]

While Timur was still in Anatolia, [Qara Yusuf](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qara_Yusuf> "Qara Yusuf") assaulted Baghdad and captured it in 1402. Timur returned to Persia and sent his grandson, Abu Bakr ibn Miran Shah, to reconquer Baghdad, which he did. Timur then spent some time in [Ardabil](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardabil> "Ardabil"), where he gave [Ali Safavi](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Safavi> "Ali Safavi"), leader of the [Safaviyya](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safaviyya> "Safaviyya"), several captives. Subsequently, he marched to Khorasan and then to Samarkhand, where he spent nine months celebrating and preparing to invade Mongolia and China.[116]

## Attempts to attack the Ming dynasty

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/The_campaign_against_China._Shah_Rukh_leads_the_Timurid_army_towards_China_on_19_February_1405%2C_after_Timur%27s_death._Facing_folios_414r%2C_413v.jpg/330px-The_campaign_against_China._Shah_Rukh_leads_the_Timurid_army_towards_China_on_19_February_1405%2C_after_Timur%27s_death._Facing_folios_414r%2C_413v.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_campaign_against_China._Shah_Rukh_leads_the_Timurid_army_towards_China_on_19_February_1405,_after_Timur%27s_death._Facing_folios_414r,_413v.jpg>)The campaign against China. [Shah Rukh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Rukh> "Shah Rukh") (right) leads the Timurid army towards China on 19 February 1405, after Timur's death. _Zafarnama_ (1436)

In 1368, the [Yuan dynasty](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty> "Yuan dynasty") collapsed and was succeeded by the [Ming dynasty](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty> "Ming dynasty"). The Ming dynasty during the reigns of its founder, the [Hongwu Emperor](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongwu_Emperor> "Hongwu Emperor"), and his son, the [Yongle Emperor](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongle_Emperor> "Yongle Emperor"), produced tributary states of many Central Asian countries. In 1394, the Hongwu Emperor's ambassadors eventually presented Timur with a letter addressing him as a subject. Timur had the ambassadors [Fu An](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu_An> "Fu An"), Guo Ji, and Liu Wei detained.[117] Neither the Hongwu Emperor's next ambassador, Chen Dewen (1397), nor the delegation announcing the accession of the Yongle Emperor fared any better.[117]

Timur eventually planned to invade China. To this end, Timur made an alliance with surviving Mongol tribes in the [Mongolian Plateau](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_Plateau> "Mongolian Plateau") and prepared all the way to [Bukhara](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukhara> "Bukhara"). [Engke Khan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engke_Khan> "Engke Khan") sent his grandson [Öljei Temür Khan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96ljei_Tem%C3%BCr_Khan> "Öljei Temür Khan"), also known as "Buyanshir Khan" after he converted to Islam while at the court of Timur in Samarkand.[118]

## Death

Timur preferred to fight his battles in the spring. However, he moved east via [Timur's Gates](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur%27s_Gates> "Timur's Gates") and died en route during an uncharacteristic winter campaign. In December 1404, Timur began military campaigns against Ming China and detained a Ming envoy. He became ill while encamped on the farther side of the Syr Daria and died at [Farab](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farab> "Farab") on 17 or 18 February 1405,[119] before ever reaching the Chinese border.[120] After his death, the Ming envoys such as [Fu An](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu_An> "Fu An") and the remaining entourage were released[117] by his grandson [Khalil Sultan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalil_Sultan> "Khalil Sultan"). 

Geographer [Clements Markham](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clements_Markham> "Clements Markham"), in his introduction to the narrative of Clavijo's embassy, states that, after Timur died, his body "was [embalmed](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embalmed> "Embalmed") with [musk](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musk> "Musk") and [rose water](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_water> "Rose water"), wrapped in [linen](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linen> "Linen"), laid in an [ebony](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebony> "Ebony") coffin and sent to Samarkand, where it was buried".[121] His tomb, the [Gur-e-Amir](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gur-e-Amir> "Gur-e-Amir"), still stands in Samarkand, though it has been heavily restored in recent years.[122]

## Succession

Main article: [Timurid Empire](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire> "Timurid Empire")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/ShrineofAmirTimur.jpg/250px-ShrineofAmirTimur.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ShrineofAmirTimur.jpg>)[Timur's mausoleum](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gur-e-Amir> "Gur-e-Amir") is located in [Samarkand](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarkand> "Samarkand"), [Uzbekistan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan> "Uzbekistan").

Timur had twice previously appointed an heir apparent to succeed him, both of whom he had outlived. The first, his son [Jahangir](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahangir_Mirza_\(Timurid_prince\)> "Jahangir Mirza \(Timurid prince\)"), died of illness in 1376.[123][124]: 51 The second, his grandson [Muhammad Sultan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Sultan_Mirza> "Muhammad Sultan Mirza"), had died from battle wounds in 1403.[125] After the latter's death, Timur did nothing to replace him. It was only when he was on his own death-bed that he appointed Muhammad Sultan's younger brother, [Pir Muhammad](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pir_Muhammad_ibn_Jahangir> "Pir Muhammad ibn Jahangir") as his successor.[126]

Pir Muhammad was unable to gain sufficient support from his relatives and a bitter civil war erupted amongst Timur's descendants, with multiple princes pursuing their claims. It was not until 1409 that Timur's youngest son, [Shah Rukh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Rukh> "Shah Rukh") was able to overcome his rivals and take the throne as Timur's successor.[127]

## Religious views

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Timur_at_the_funerals_of_his_grandson_Muhammad_Sultan_Mirza_ibn_Jahangir_in_1403._Zafarnama_of_1436.jpg/250px-Timur_at_the_funerals_of_his_grandson_Muhammad_Sultan_Mirza_ibn_Jahangir_in_1403._Zafarnama_of_1436.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Timur_at_the_funerals_of_his_grandson_Muhammad_Sultan_Mirza_ibn_Jahangir_in_1403._Zafarnama_of_1436.jpg>)Timur at the funerals of his grandson [Muhammad Sultan Mirza ibn Jahangir](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Sultan_Mirza> "Muhammad Sultan Mirza") in 1403. _Zafarnama_ (1436)

Timur was a practising [Sunni Muslim](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim> "Sunni Muslim"), possibly belonging to the [Naqshbandi Order](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naqshbandi_Order> "Naqshbandi Order") of [Sufism](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism> "Sufism"), which was influential in Transoxiana.[128] His chief official religious counsellor and adviser was the [Hanafi](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafi> "Hanafi") scholar Abd al-Jabbar Khwarezmi. In [Termez](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termez> "Termez"), he had come under the influence of the spiritual mentor [Sayyid Baraka](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Baraka> "Sayyid Baraka"), a leader from [Balkh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkh> "Balkh") who is buried alongside Timur in [Gur-e-Amir](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gur-e-Amir> "Gur-e-Amir").[129][130][131]

Timur was known to hold [Ali](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali> "Ali") and the [Ahl al-Bayt](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahl_al-Bayt> "Ahl al-Bayt") in high regard and has been noted by various scholars for his "pro-[Shia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam> "Shia Islam")" stance. However, he also punished Shias for desecrating the memories of the [Companions of the Prophet](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companions_of_the_Prophet> "Companions of the Prophet").[132] Timur was also noted for attacking the Shia with Sunni apologism, while at other times he attacked Sunnis on religious grounds as well.[133] In contrast, Timur held the [Seljuk](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire> "Seljuk Empire") Sultan [Ahmad Sanjar](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Sanjar> "Ahmad Sanjar") in high regard for attacking the [Ismailis](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isma%27ilism> "Isma'ilism") at [Alamut](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamut_Castle> "Alamut Castle"), and Timur's own attack on Ismailis at [Anjudan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anjudan> "Anjudan") was equally brutal.[133]

## Personality

Timur is regarded as a military genius and as a brilliant tactician with an uncanny ability to work within a highly fluid political structure to win and maintain a loyal following of nomads during his rule in Central Asia. He was also considered extraordinarily intelligent – not only intuitively but also intellectually.[134] In Samarkand and his many travels, Timur, under the guidance of distinguished scholars, was able to learn Persian, Mongolian, and Turkic languages[135] (according to [ibn Arabshah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Arabshah> "Ibn Arabshah"), Timur could not speak [Arabic](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic> "Arabic")).[136] However, Timur held Persian in distinction, as it was the language not only of his court, but also that of his chancellery.[137]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3b/Timur_leading_his_troops_at_the_1401_Siege_of_Baghdad._Near-contemporary_manuscript_commissioned_by_his_grandson_Ibrahim_Sultan_in_1424%E2%80%9328._Published_in_1435-36.jpg/250px-thumbnail.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Timur_leading_his_troops_at_the_1401_Siege_of_Baghdad._Near-contemporary_manuscript_commissioned_by_his_grandson_Ibrahim_Sultan_in_1424%E2%80%9328._Published_in_1435-36.jpg>)Timur leading his troops at the 1401 [Siege of Baghdad](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Baghdad_\(1401\)> "Siege of Baghdad \(1401\)"). Near-contemporary portrait in [_Zafarnama_](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafarnama_\(Yazdi_biography\)> "Zafarnama \(Yazdi biography\)"), commissioned by his grandson [Ibrahim Sultan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Sultan_\(Timurid\)> "Ibrahim Sultan \(Timurid\)") in 1424–28. Published in 1435–36.

According to [John Joseph Saunders](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Joseph_Saunders> "John Joseph Saunders"), Timur was "the product of an Islamized and Iranized society" rather than a [steppe nomad](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe_nomad> "Steppe nomad").[138] More importantly, Timur was characterized as an opportunist. Taking advantage of his Turco-Mongolian heritage, Timur frequently used either the Islam or the [sharia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharia> "Sharia"), [fiqh](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiqh> "Fiqh"), and traditions of the Mongol Empire to achieve his military goals or domestic political aims.[9] Timur was a learned king, and enjoyed the company of scholars; he was tolerant and generous to them. He was a contemporary of the Persian poet [Hafez](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafez> "Hafez"), and a story of their meeting explains that Timur summoned Hafez, who had written a [ghazal](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazal> "Ghazal") with the following verse: 

    For the black mole on thy cheek
    I would give the cities of Samarkand and Bukhara.

Timur upbraided him for this verse and said, "By the blows of my well tempered sword I have conquered the greater part of the world to enlarge [Samarkand](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarkand> "Samarkand") and [Bukhara](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukhara> "Bukhara"), my capitals and residences; and you, pitiful creature, would exchange these two cities for a mole." Hafez, undaunted, replied, "It is by similar generosity that I have been reduced, as you see, to my present state of poverty." It is reported that he was pleased by the witty answer, and the poet departed with magnificent gifts.[139][140]

There is a widely held view that Timur's real motive for his campaigns was imperial ambition, as expressed in his statement: "The whole expanse of the inhabited part of the world is not large enough to have two kings." However, beyond Iran, Timur plundered the states he invaded to enrich his native Samarqand and neglected the conquered areas, which may have led to the relatively rapid disintegration of his Empire after his death.[141]

Timur used Persian expressions in his conversations often, and his motto was the Persian phrase _rāstī rustī_ (راستی رستی, meaning "truth is safety" or _"veritas salus"_).[136] He is credited with the invention of the [Tamerlane chess](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamerlane_chess> "Tamerlane chess") variant, played on a 10×11 board.[142]

## Exchanges with Europe

Main article: [Timurid relations with Europe](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_relations_with_Europe> "Timurid relations with Europe")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Letter_of_Tamerlane_to_Charles_VI_1402.jpg/250px-Letter_of_Tamerlane_to_Charles_VI_1402.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Letter_of_Tamerlane_to_Charles_VI_1402.jpg>)Letter of Timur to [Charles VI of France](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI_of_France> "Charles VI of France"), 1402, a witness to [Timurid relations with Europe](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_relations_with_Europe> "Timurid relations with Europe"). [Archives Nationales](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archives_Nationales_\(France\)> "Archives Nationales \(France\)"), Paris.

Timur had numerous [epistolary](<https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/epistolary> "wikt:epistolary") and diplomatic exchanges with various European states, especially Spain and France. Relations between the court of [Henry III of Castile](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_III_of_Castile> "Henry III of Castile") and that of Timur played an important part in medieval [Castilian](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile> "Crown of Castile") diplomacy. In 1402, the time of the [Battle of Ankara](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ankara> "Battle of Ankara"), two Spanish ambassadors were already with Timur: Pelayo de Sotomayor and Fernando de Palazuelos. Later, Timur sent to the court of the [Kingdom of León and Castile](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Le%C3%B3n#León_and_Castile> "Kingdom of León") a Chagatai ambassador named Hajji Muhammad al-Qazi with letters and gifts. 

In return, Henry III of Castile sent a famous embassy to Timur's court in Samarkand in 1403–06, led by [Ruy González de Clavijo](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruy_Gonz%C3%A1lez_de_Clavijo> "Ruy González de Clavijo"), with two other ambassadors, Alfonso Paez and Gomez de Salazar. On their return, Timur affirmed that he regarded the king of Castile "as his very own son". 

According to Clavijo, Timur's good treatment of the Spanish delegation contrasted with the disdain shown by his host toward the envoys of the "lord of [Cathay](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathay> "Cathay")" (i.e., the Yongle Emperor), the Chinese ruler. Clavijo's visit to Samarkand allowed him to report to the European audience on the news from Cathay (China), which few Europeans had been able to visit directly in the century that had passed since the travels of [Marco Polo](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo> "Marco Polo"). 

The French archives preserve: 

  * A 30 July 1402 letter from Timur to [Charles VI of France](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI_of_France> "Charles VI of France"), suggesting that he send traders to Asia. It is written in [Persian](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language> "Persian language").[143]
  * A May 1403 letter. This is a Latin transcription of a letter from Timur to Charles VI, and another from [Miran Shah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miran_Shah> "Miran Shah"), his son, to the Christian princes, announcing their victory over [Bayezid I](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayezid_I> "Bayezid I") at [Smyrna](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smyrna> "Smyrna").[144]

A copy has been kept of the answer of Charles VI to Timur, dated 15 June 1403.[145]

In addition, Byzantine [John VII Palaiologos](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_VII_Palaiologos> "John VII Palaiologos") who was a regent during his uncle's absence in the West, sent a [Dominican friar](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order> "Dominican Order") in August 1401 to Timur, to pay his respect and propose paying tribute to him instead of the Turks, once he managed to defeat them.[102]

## Legacy

Timur's legacy is a mixed one. While Central Asia blossomed under his reign, other places, such as [Baghdad](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad#Stagnation_and_invasions_\(10th–16th_centuries\)> "Baghdad"), [Damascus](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus#Timurlank> "Damascus"), [Delhi](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi> "Delhi") and other [Arab](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab> "Arab"), [Georgian](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur%27s_invasions_of_Georgia> "Timur's invasions of Georgia"), [Persian](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians> "Persians"), and [Indian](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_people> "Indian people") cities were sacked and destroyed and their populations massacred. Thus, while Timur still retains a positive image in [Muslim](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim> "Muslim") [Central Asia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia> "Central Asia"), he is vilified by many in [Arabia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia> "Arabia"), [Iraq](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq> "Iraq"), [Persia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia> "Persia"), and [India](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India> "India"), where some of his greatest atrocities were carried out. However, [Ibn Khaldun](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Khaldun> "Ibn Khaldun") praises Timur for having unified much of the Muslim world when other conquerors of the time could not.[146] The next great conqueror of the [Middle East](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East> "Middle East"), [Nader Shah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah> "Nader Shah"), was greatly influenced by Timur and almost re-enacted Timur's conquests and battle strategies in his [own campaigns](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naderian_Wars> "Naderian Wars"). Like Timur, Nader Shah conquered most of [Caucasia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Campaign_\(1735\)> "Caucasus Campaign \(1735\)"), [Persia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaigns_of_Nader_Shah> "Campaigns of Nader Shah"), and [Central Asia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah%27s_Central_Asian_campaign> "Nader Shah's Central Asian campaign") along with also [sacking Delhi](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah%27s_invasion_of_India> "Nader Shah's invasion of India").[147]

Timur's short-lived empire also melded the [Turko-Persian tradition](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turco-Persian_tradition> "Turco-Persian tradition") in [Transoxiana](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transoxiana> "Transoxiana"), and, in most of the territories that he incorporated into his [fiefdom](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiefdom> "Fiefdom"), [Persian](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language> "Persian language") became the primary [language](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language> "Language") of administration and literary culture (_[diwan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divan> "Divan")_), regardless of [ethnicity](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity> "Ethnicity").[148] In addition, during his reign, some contributions to Turkic literature were penned, with Turkic cultural influence expanding and flourishing as a result. A literary form of [Chagatai Turkic](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_language> "Chagatai language") came into use alongside Persian as both a cultural and an [official language](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language> "Official language").[149]

Tamerlane virtually exterminated the [Church of the East](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East> "Church of the East"), which had previously been a major branch of [Christianity](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity> "Christianity") but afterwards became largely confined to a small area now known as the [Assyrian Triangle](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Triangle> "Assyrian Triangle").[150]

In Uzbekistan, Timur is referred to as the "Founder of the Uzbek nation" despite having belonged to a competing tribe that hated the Uzbeks.[151] His monument in [Tashkent](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashkent> "Tashkent") now occupies the place where [Karl Marx](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx> "Karl Marx")'s statue once stood. The [Amir Timur Museum](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Timur_Museum> "Amir Timur Museum") in Tashkent focuses on his genealogy and life. 

In 1794, [Sake Dean Mahomed](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sake_Dean_Mahomed> "Sake Dean Mahomed") published his travel book, _The Travels of Dean Mahomet_. The book begins with the praise of [Genghis Khan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan> "Genghis Khan"), Timur, and particularly the first [Mughal emperor](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_emperor> "Mughal emperor"), [Babur](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur> "Babur"). He also gives important details on the then incumbent [Mughal Emperor](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Emperor> "Mughal Emperor") [Shah Alam II](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Alam_II> "Shah Alam II"). 

The poem "[Tamerlane](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamerlane_\(poem\)> "Tamerlane \(poem\)")" by [Edgar Allan Poe](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe> "Edgar Allan Poe") follows a fictionalized version of Timur's life. 

### Historical sources

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7a/Autograph_of_Ahmad_ibn_Arabshah.png/250px-Autograph_of_Ahmad_ibn_Arabshah.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Autograph_of_Ahmad_ibn_Arabshah.png>)[Ahmad ibn Arabshah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Arabshah> "Ahmad ibn Arabshah")'s work on the _Life of Timur_

The earliest known history of his reign was [Nizam al-Din Shami](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizam_al-Din_Shami> "Nizam al-Din Shami")'s _[Zafarnama](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafarnama_\(Shami_biography\)> "Zafarnama \(Shami biography\)")_ , which was written during Timur's lifetime. Between 1424 and 1428, [Sharaf ad-Din Ali Yazdi](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharaf_ad-Din_Ali_Yazdi> "Sharaf ad-Din Ali Yazdi") wrote a second _[Zafarnama](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafarnama_\(Yazdi_biography\)> "Zafarnama \(Yazdi biography\)")_ drawing heavily on Shami's earlier work. [Ahmad ibn Arabshah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Arabshah> "Ahmad ibn Arabshah") wrote a much less favorable history in Arabic. Arabshah's history was translated into [Latin](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin> "Latin") by the [Dutch](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people> "Dutch people") [Orientalist](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientalism> "Orientalism") [Jacobus Golius](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobus_Golius> "Jacobus Golius") in 1636. 

As Timurid-sponsored histories, the two _Zafarnama_ s present a dramatically different picture from Arabshah's chronicle. [William Jones](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Jones_\(philologist\)> "William Jones \(philologist\)") remarked that the former presented Timur as a "liberal, benevolent and illustrious prince" while the latter painted him as "deformed and impious, of a low birth and detestable principles".[62]

#### _Malfuzat-i Timuri_

The _Malfuzat-i Timurī_ and the appended _Tuzūk-i Tīmūrī_ , supposedly Timur's own autobiography, are almost certainly 17th-century fabrications.[30][152] The scholar Abu Taleb Hosayni presented the texts to the [Mughal](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire> "Mughal Empire") emperor [Shah Jahan](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Jahan> "Shah Jahan"), a distant descendant of Timur, in 1637–1638, supposedly after discovering the [Chagatai language](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagatai_language> "Chagatai language") originals in the library of a [Yemeni](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen> "Yemen") ruler. Due to the distance between Yemen and Timur's base in Transoxiana and the lack of any other evidence of the originals, most historians consider the story highly implausible, and suspect Hosayni of inventing both the text and its origin story.[152]

### European views

Timur arguably had a significant impact on the [Renaissance](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance> "Renaissance") culture and early modern Europe.[153] European views of Timur were mixed throughout the fifteenth century, with some European countries calling him an ally and others seeing him as a threat to Europe because of his rapid expansion and brutality.[154]: 341

When Timur captured the Ottoman Sultan [Bayezid](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayezid_I> "Bayezid I") at [Ankara](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara> "Ankara"), he was often praised and seen as a trusted ally by European rulers, such as [Charles VI of France](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI_of_France> "Charles VI of France") and [Henry IV of England](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_IV_of_England> "Henry IV of England"), because they believed he was saving Christianity from the Turkic Empire in the Middle East. Those two kings also praised him because his victory at Ankara allowed Christian merchants to remain in the Middle East and allowed for their safe return home to both [France](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France> "France") and [England](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England> "England"). Timur was also praised because it was believed that he helped restore the right of passage for [Christian pilgrims](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage> "Christian pilgrimage") to the [Holy Land](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land> "Holy Land").[154]: 341–344

Other Europeans viewed Timur as a barbaric enemy who presented a threat to both European culture and the religion of Christianity. His rise to power moved many leaders, such as [Henry III of Castile](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_III_of_Castile> "Henry III of Castile"), to send embassies to [Samarkand](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarkand> "Samarkand") to scout out Timur, learn about his people, make alliances with him, and try to convince him to convert to Christianity in order to avoid war.[154]: 348–349

In the introduction to a 1723 translation of Yazdi's _Zafarnama_ , the translator wrote:[155]

> [[M. Petis de la Croix](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_P%C3%A9tis_de_la_Croix> "François Pétis de la Croix")] tells us, that there are calumnies and impostures, which have been published by authors of romances, and Turkish writers who were his enemies, and envious at his glory: among whom is [Ahmed Bin Arabschah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Arabshah> "Ahmad ibn Arabshah") ... As Timur-Bec had conquered the Turks and Arabians of Syria, and had even taken the [Sultan Bajazet](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayezid_I> "Bayezid I") prisoner, it is no wonder that he has been misrepresented by the historians of those nations, who, in despite of truth, and against the dignity of history, have fallen into great excesses on this subject.

### Exhumation and alleged curse

Main article: [Curse of Timur](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_of_Timur> "Curse of Timur")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Tomb_of_Timur%2C_Gur-e-Amir.jpg/250px-Tomb_of_Timur%2C_Gur-e-Amir.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tomb_of_Timur,_Gur-e-Amir.jpg>)Tomb of Timur in [Gur-e-Amir](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gur-e-Amir> "Gur-e-Amir"), [Samarkand](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarkand> "Samarkand")

Timur's body was [exhumed](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhumation> "Exhumation") from his tomb on 19 June 1941 and his remains examined by the [Soviet](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR> "USSR") [anthropologists](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology> "Anthropology") [Mikhail M. Gerasimov](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Mikhaylovich_Gerasimov> "Mikhail Mikhaylovich Gerasimov"), [Lev V. Oshanin](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_V._Oshanin> "Lev V. Oshanin") and V. Ia. Zezenkova. Gerasimov reconstructed the likeness of Timur from his skull and found that his facial characteristics displayed "typical [Mongoloid](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongoloid> "Mongoloid") features", i.e. [East Asian](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_people> "East Asian people") in modern terms.[156][157][158] An anthropologic study of Timur's cranium shows that he belonged predominately to the "South Siberian Mongoloid type".[159] At 5 feet 8 inches (173 centimeters), Timur was tall for his era. The examinations confirmed that Timur was [lame](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limp> "Limp") and had a withered right arm due to his injuries. His right thighbone had knitted together with his kneecap, and the configuration of the knee joint suggests that he kept his leg bent at all times and therefore would have had a pronounced limp.[160] He appears to have been broad-chested and his hair and beard were red.[161]

It is alleged that Timur's tomb was inscribed with the words, "When I rise from the dead, the world shall tremble". It is also said that when Gerasimov exhumed the body, an additional inscription inside the casket was found, which read, "Whomsoever [_[sic](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic> "Sic")_] opens my tomb shall unleash an invader more terrible than I."[162] Even though people close to Gerasimov claim that this story is a fabrication, it became known as the [Curse of Timur](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_of_Timur> "Curse of Timur"). In any case, three days after Gerasimov began the exhumation, [Germany](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany> "Nazi Germany") [invaded the Soviet Union](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa> "Operation Barbarossa"), which resulted in one of the [deadliest invasions in human history](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_casualties_of_the_Soviet_Union> "World War II casualties of the Soviet Union").[163] Timur was re-buried with full Islamic ritual in November 1942, just before the Soviet victory at the [Battle of Stalingrad](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad> "Battle of Stalingrad").[9]
