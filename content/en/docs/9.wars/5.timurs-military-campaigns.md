title: Timur's Military Campaigns (1370–1405)
description: Turco-Mongol campaigns of Timur that briefly forged a vast Eurasian empire, crippled rivals, and catalyzed a Timurid cultural florescence.
---

## Historical setting

In the fragmented post-Mongol political landscape of Transoxiana, Timur (Tamerlane) rose from a Barlas tribal base to claim guardianship of Chinggisid legitimacy while ruling as amir. Between 1370 and 1405 he conducted campaigns across Iran, the Caucasus, the Golden Horde’s western steppes, northern India, Syria, and Anatolia. His armies fused Turkic-Mongol military traditions with sophisticated logistics and terror as a strategic instrument.

## Principal campaigns

Repeated wars against Tokhtamysh devastated Golden Horde centers and redirected steppe politics. The 1398–1399 Indian campaign culminated in the sack of Delhi. Operations against the Mamluks and the 1402 victory over Bayezid I at Ankara temporarily shattered Ottoman consolidation. Capitals such as Samarkand became repositories of plundered artisans and manuscripts, fueling architectural and intellectual patronage.

## Outcomes and significance

Timur’s empire lacked durable institutional succession; after his death it fragmented among Timurid princes. Nevertheless, the campaigns reshaped power balances: the Golden Horde’s decline accelerated, Ottoman recovery was delayed, and a Persianate Timurid cultural sphere flourished in Central Asia and eastern Iran. For Turkic history, Timur exemplifies the late medieval Turco-Mongol conqueror whose legacy was as much cultural as territorial.

## Historiographical notes

Official Timurid historiography (e.g., *Zafarnama* traditions) glorifies destiny; Ottoman, Mamluk, and Rus’ sources record catastrophe. Modern historians debate the balance between calculated state-building and predatory extraction.
