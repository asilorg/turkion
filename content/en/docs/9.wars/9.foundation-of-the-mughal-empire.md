title: Foundation of the Mughal Empire (1526)
description: Babur’s victory over the Delhi Sultanate, using gunpowder tactics, established a Turco-Mongol Timurid dynasty that transformed South Asian politics and culture.
---

## Historical setting

Zahir al-Din Muhammad Babur, a Timurid prince of Turco-Mongol lineage displaced from Central Asian patrimonies, turned toward Hindustan after setbacks in Ferghana and Samarkand. Northern India under the Lodi Afghan dynasty faced elite factionalism. Babur’s force, though numerically inferior, deployed field artillery and tulughma flanking tactics associated with Ottoman and Central Asian military innovation.

## The 1526 campaign

At the First Battle of Panipat (21 April 1526), Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi. The victory opened Delhi and Agra and inaugurated Mughal rule. Subsequent battles—notably Khanwa (1527) against Rana Sanga—secured the nascent regime against Rajput coalitions. Babur’s memoirs (*Baburnama*) provide a rare first-person Timurid perspective on conquest, ecology, and court culture.

## Outcomes and significance

The Mughal Empire became one of the early modern world’s most powerful states, synthesizing Persianate administration, Turkic-Mongol dynastic ideology, and Indian social realities. For Turkic history, Panipat illustrates the long reach of Timurid lineages beyond Central Asia and the export of steppe-Islamic military and cultural templates into South Asia. Architecture, painting, and language politics under later Mughals left enduring regional legacies.

## Historiographical notes

Indo-Persian chronicles, Rajput traditions, and European travelers frame the foundation differently. Scholarship debates continuity with the Delhi Sultanate versus Timurid rupture, and the degree to which “gunpowder empire” models explain Mughal success.
